# Rookery

**A small page for the colony that comes back to the same elms year after year.**

A rookery is a colony of rooks — sometimes a dozen pairs, sometimes
three hundred — built in the high forks of the same beeches or elms,
year on year, generation on generation. The birds patch last year's
cup with new twigs in March, raise their clutch in April, and by mid-
June the canopy is loud with the brown of fledged young. By August
the rookery has gone quiet again. Next spring the same birds (or
their daughters) come back to the same fork in the same tree.

## The pitch in one breath

Most work is one bird trying to remember the way home to a single
tree alone. The rookery is the other thing. The rookery is what
keeps coming back. Each year the same fork in the same elm gets
patched again with new twigs, and the pile grows another inch
deeper, and the colony grows another nest broader, until the
parish has a landmark made entirely of returning birds.

## The four perches

- **Twig** — a single twig wedged in the fork: the first half-page,
  the empty notebook with one entry, the line of code that is the
  only line of code. Not yet a nest. Could blow down in a wind.
- **Cup** — a finished nest, one season's work: the first draft, the
  first complete piece of practice, the first finished chapter.
  One pair's worth. Holds a clutch.
- **Bough** — several nests in one tree, a small clan: a body of
  work, a small archive, a corpus the same hands have built up
  patiently over a few years. Visibly inhabited.
- **Rookery** — the full colony, twenty trees, three hundred nests,
  a parish landmark: a lifework, a tradition, a school of practice,
  generations of the same returning labour, visible from a mile off.

## The four states

- **Roosting** — settled in for the night, the trees full but the
  parliament quiet. The work is held. Nothing being added. Nothing
  leaving.
- **Calling** — vocal at dawn, calling and answering, the sentry on
  a low branch. The work is being done — talking to itself, sounding
  out, alive.
- **Mending** — the birds are patching the cup with fresh twigs
  brought up from the wood below. Maintenance. The slow upkeep that
  keeps the colony from blowing apart in November.
- **Empty** — the rookery has been left for the season. The cups
  stand bare against the sky. Some will be patched again in March;
  some will not.

## What's in this folder

- `index.html` — the landing page, with a stand of high elms, three
  hundred nests rough-pencilled against a dusk sky, and a small
  circle of rooks coming back in for the roost.
- `styles.css` — a rookery palette: dusk, hawk-light, elm, bark,
  rook, twig, lichen, eggshell, sentry; Fraunces for headings,
  Inter for body, JetBrains Mono for the perch counts.
- `script.js` — twelve nests seeded across a small parish rookery in
  the middle of June 2026. A cathedral nest in the old elm by the
  church tower since 1842, a twig in the corner ash from a young
  pair this March, the empty cup in the felled hawthorn that no one
  will patch again.

## Running it

Open `index.html` in a browser. No build step.
