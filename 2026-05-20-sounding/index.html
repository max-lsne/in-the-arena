How deep are you
in the work?

The streak app counts the days. The to-do app counts the tasks. Neither of them knows the difference between the week you were lost in the desk and the week you opened the file three times and closed it.

That difference is the whole game. Sounding is the smallest tool we could think of for noticing it. Five questions, once a week, scored low to high. The line you build is the floor of the project.

How it works

  1. 01

    Name one long thing

    One project. Long. The kind you measure in months. The book. The thesis. The desk in the garage.

  2. 02

    Drop a line each week

    Five facets, one to five. Sixty seconds. Sunday is a good day, but any day is the right one.

  3. 03

    Name the bottom

    One word — sand, rock, kelp, no bottom — for what the floor felt like this week.

  4. 04

    Watch the seabed

    Each week the chart grows. The deep stretches and the shallow ones. The storms. The long climbs back.

The four bottoms

  • Sand. Easy. Found. The work sat where you put it.
  • Rock. Hard but holding. Deep work that did not slip.
  • Kelp. Tangled. A week pulling at knots.
  • No bottom. The line did not catch. A storm, or a wait.

Who takes a sounding

The novelist in a third draft

The book is nine months in. Some weeks she's underwater with it. Some she barely opens the file. The chart tells the truth her to-do list keeps swallowing.

The PhD student in chapter nine

Eighteen months in, the viva is a hill, not a deadline. The bearing is high, the hold is low. The seabed shows him where to put a quiet week.

The furniture maker, one piece deep

A writing desk by hand. A storm in week eleven when the panel cracked. A long flat in twelve. Surfacing in seventeen. The chart already remembers.

The founder building one thing

A single product, a year out. The deep weeks are the ones that ship. The kelp weeks are the ones that teach. Sounding is for keeping the difference.
