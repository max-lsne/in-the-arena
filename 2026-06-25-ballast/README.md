# Ballast

**A small page about carrying your steadying weight low, where no one sees it.**

Ballast is dead weight — pig-iron, stone, water — stowed low in a hull
where no passenger ever sees it and no one ever thinks to thank it. It
earns nothing on a calm day. It is the single reason the ship is still
on her feet when the squall lays her on her beam ends. The cargo people
admire rides high on the deck and makes her tender; the weight that
actually saves her sits in the bilge, in the dark, at the bottom of the
boat.

## The pitch in one breath

A ship is steadied not by how much she carries but by how low. Weight
up high — the deck cargo everyone admires — makes her tender: quick to
roll, slow to come back, a little nearer to going over each time. The
boring weight struck down into the bilge does nothing on a flat calm
and everything on the worst night of the crossing. You cannot stow
ballast in a gale; the work is done in harbour, in fair weather. Carry
it low. Lash it down early. Let the best of it become the keel.

## The four kinds of weight

- **Shingle** — loose beach gravel shovelled in for trim. Cheap,
  quick, and treacherous: it steadies her in a flat calm and runs to
  leeward the moment she heels. The new thing, not yet stowed.
- **Stone** — rough rubble stowed and chocked under the sole. Heavier,
  steadier, properly down low, but still movable. The fundamental that
  is holding, though not yet permanent.
- **Iron** — cast pig-iron struck down into the bilge and lashed.
  Dense, deliberate, deep — the boring weight that quietly rights her
  in every blow. The settled discipline the boat is steadied by.
- **Keel** — lead run into the keel when she was laid down. Ballast
  that stopped being cargo and became the boat: it never shifts, never
  has to be thought about, and always brings the mast back to the sky.

## The four ways it is stowed

- **Loose** — unstowed and shifting, apt to run to leeward in a
  knockdown and capsize the boat it was meant to save.
- **Stowed** — struck down below and placed, but not yet secured. Low,
  but it could still move in a hard sea.
- **Lashed** — chocked and lashed down low, where it stays put when she
  goes over. The work done in fair weather.
- **Righting** — settled so deep and so long that it is the thing
  bringing the mast back up to the sky. It does not have to be thought
  about; it simply rights her.

## What's in this folder

- `index.html` — the landing page, with a wooden ketch heeled by the
  wind in the Western Approaches: the squall coming up to weather, the
  glamorous deck cargo riding high, the pig-iron struck down in the
  bilge, and the lead keel low at the foot of the boat dragging the
  mast back to the sky.
- `styles.css` — a sea palette: pale haze on a falling glass, wet-deck
  slate, deep water under the keel, binnacle brass, rusting pig-iron,
  waterline kelp, sailcloth cream. Fraunces for the headings, Inter for
  the body, JetBrains Mono for the gales.
- `script.js` — twelve entries on one fictional ketch's manifest in the
  back-end of 2026: the lead in the keel, the pig-iron in the bilge,
  the fresh shingle shipped this morning, the deck cargo lashed high,
  the ballast a previous owner landed to go faster and never replaced.

## Running it

Open `index.html` in a browser. No build step.
