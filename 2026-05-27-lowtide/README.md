# Lowtide

**The shore tells the truth.**

A lowtide is the hour or two on a coast when the water draws all
the way back and the strand line shows the week. Lowtide the
page asks the same of a working week: wait for the room to go
quiet on a Friday afternoon, walk the shore alone, name the
finds, decide what you carry home. The basket is small on
purpose.

## The pitch in one breath

A retro is a meeting with a chair at the head of the table.
Lowtide is a page for the other thing — the walk you take alone
on a Friday afternoon when the water pulls back and you see
what the week actually left on the shore. Three kinds of finds.
Four states of keeping. Most things get returned.

## The three things a tide leaves

- **Find** — something useful uncovered. A note in a margin, a
  question half-answered, a thread you had lost track of. Worth
  picking up.
- **Stranding** — something the week left high and dry. A
  thread you had hoped would come in further. Maybe worth
  dragging back to water; maybe not.
- **Shell** — small and bright and not useful. A pleasant
  exchange, an unexpected sentence, a kindness with nowhere to
  go. Worth keeping anyway.

## The four states of a find

- **Noticed** — you saw it on the shore. Not yet picked up.
- **Picked up** — in your hand now. You will decide.
- **Kept** — going home with you. In the basket.
- **Returned** — back to the tide. Most things get returned,
  and the basket is the better for it.

## What's in this folder

- `index.html` — the landing page with a strand view in the
  browser.
- `styles.css` — a tidal-flat palette: paper, foam, fog, gull,
  slate, water, pool, kelp, wrack, cedar, salt-sun, shell,
  mussel; Fraunces for headings, Inter for body, JetBrains Mono
  for marginalia.
- `script.js` — ten seeded finds for an imaginary last week of
  May 2026 at a bookbinder's bench in a coast town. The keepers
  lean toward two threads worth pulling into next week; the
  strandings and shells lean around them.

## Running it

Open `index.html` in a browser. No build step.
