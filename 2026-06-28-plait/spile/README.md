# Spile

**A small page about broaching only the few works you can actually draw
down — spiled, drawn slow, racked off bright — and letting the rest stay
bunged and sound rather than open to the air and souring.**

A spile is the soft little peg you ease into the shive of a cask — not to
open it wide, but to let it breathe a hair before you tap it and draw.
Broach the whole cellar at once, knock every bung out and let the air at
all of it, and by the weekend the lot is fretting, working over, turning
to vinegar together. Ease a spile into a few, draw them a glass at a time,
drop them bright and rack them off clear into the keeping cask, and they
hold sound for years. The cellar is not measured by how many casks you
broach. It is measured by how many you bring down sound.

## The pitch in one breath

You treat the cellar like a tap-room on a feast day — knock every bung
out, broach the whole stock at once, let the air in everywhere. Forty
works stand open to the air together, and not one of them is being drawn
with care. A spile is the opposite habit, on purpose: a peg, not a mallet.
You broach only what you can keep an eye on, you draw a glass at a time,
you drop them bright and rack them off clear, and you let the rest of the
stock stay bunged and sound rather than open and souring. It feels like
doing less. It is doing less, of fewer casks, and far more for the few you
have laid down to draw. Lay down what you can tend. Draw it slow. Let the
rest stay bunged.

## The hard floor

The stillage holds six casks, and no more. When it is full, the next cask
does not come down — it stays bunged in the back of the cellar until its
year comes round. The cap is the point, not a flaw: a cellar that broaches
everything keeps nothing sound.

## How far it is drawn down

- **Bunged** — stoppered and sound, not yet started. New, sealed, and
  waiting in the back of the cellar.
- **Spiled** — a soft spile eased into the shive, letting it breathe a
  hair before the tap. Begun.
- **Drawing** — the tap driven, drawing a glass at a time, slow. In use,
  and being worked down properly.
- **Racked** — dropped bright and racked off clear into the keeping cask.
  The one cured and kept; the one that matters most.

## What the cask is doing

- **Flat** — unconditioned and lifeless, or fretting if neglected. Wants
  tending before it goes off.
- **Working** — lively, fermenting, working hard. The active stage, full
  of life and needing a watch.
- **Clearing** — dropping bright, the lees falling out of it. Coming good,
  settling toward sound.
- **Sound** — clear and sound, keeps for years. Needs nothing now; it
  simply holds.

## What's in this folder

- `index.html` — the landing page, with a row of casks lying on a wooden
  stillage in the cool of the cellar: one bunged and sound, one with a
  soft spile in the top breathing a bubble or two, one with a tap drawing
  a glass below, the soft brick arch behind — and off to the side a couple
  of casks broached all at once with the froth blowing over, the wrong
  way.
- `styles.css` — a cider-cellar palette: pale cider straw in the light,
  russet and cask-oak amber for the staves, cellar-dark oak in the shade,
  cider gold drawn into the glass, apple-leaf green for a cask gone sound,
  and the sharp turning red-brown of one left to sour. Fraunces for the
  headings, Inter for the body, JetBrains Mono for the condition.
- `script.js` — one fictional cellar ("Crabtree cellar") partway through a
  year, with a handful of casks at different stages and conditions. A live
  *cellar line* reads the whole cellar at once: it lies level and clear
  when a few casks are drawn slow, and rolls, frets and turns as the air
  gets in — too many broached at once, or too many left to sour.
  Keyboard-first, and it remembers your cellar between visits.

## Running it

Open `index.html` in a browser. No build step.
