# Heddle

**A small page about raising only the threads you mean to cross now,
working them to a true selvage, and letting the rest of the warp hang
slack off the beam until their turn.**

A heddle is the small frame that lifts a chosen handful of warp threads
and opens the shed — the clean triangular gap the shuttle flies through.
You cannot lift the whole warp at once and still weave: raise everything
and there is no gap, no pass, no cloth, only a taut wall of held thread.
So the heddle raises the chosen few, the shed opens, the pick goes
through, and the reed beats it home. The rest of the warp hangs slack off
the beam, untouched and unharmed, waiting its turn. The web does not build
because you held up every thread; it builds because a few were raised,
crossed, and beaten true.

## The pitch in one breath

You try to lift the whole warp at once — every message, every
half-promise, every someday-project pulled taut and held up together, the
full width of the beam standing in the air at the same moment. And there
is no shed: no gap, no pass, nowhere for the shuttle to go. A heddle is the
opposite discipline, on purpose. It lifts a few ends and leaves the rest.
You raise only the handful you mean to cross now, the shed opens clean, the
shuttle flies through, the reed beats the weft home — and the threads you
did not raise hang slack off the beam, waiting. It feels like doing less.
It is doing less of more threads, and far more of the few you actually
raised. Raise the chosen few. Pass the pick. Beat it home. Let the rest
hang.

## The hard floor

The shed holds six raised ends, and no more. When it is full, the next
thread is not raised — it hangs slack off the beam until an end already up
is beaten in and crossed. The cap is the point, not a flaw: a shed that
tries to hold the whole warp opens for nothing and weaves no cloth.

## How far through the loom

- **Slack** — a warp end hanging loose off the beam, untensioned, not yet
  drawn through. New, and not yet placed.
- **Threaded** — drawn through its heddle eye and the reed, set in its
  place on the loom. Placed, not yet lifted.
- **Raised** — lifted by the heddle into the open shed, up in the gap and
  ready for the pick. In hand.
- **Crossed** — the weft passed and beaten, woven into the web for good;
  the thread that became cloth. The one that matters most.

## What the work is doing

- **Loose** — slack and untensioned, frays and tangles in the hand. Wants
  taking up before it knots.
- **Drawn** — tensioned and drawn even across the warp. Coming right under
  the hand.
- **Picked** — the pick thrown through clean and lying true. Going well,
  the cloth growing.
- **Beaten** — beaten home tight into the web with the reed. Set; needs
  nothing now — it simply holds.

## What's in this folder

- `index.html` — the landing page, with a loom dressed and working: the
  whole warp of parallel threads stretched down the beam, the heddle frame
  lifting a chosen few of them to open a clean triangular shed, the shuttle
  passing through with the weft, the woven web building below with a
  straight true selvage edge, and a few ends off to the side hanging slack
  and tangled — the un-raised many, waiting their turn.
- `styles.css` — a dyer's palette: unbleached linen for the ground, indigo
  and woad for the cool primary, deep indigo in the shade, straw-gold for
  the lifted thread, madder red for the slack end and the fraying edge, and
  flax-water teal for the selvage line. Fraunces for the headings, Inter
  for the body, JetBrains Mono for the loom's reckoning.
- `script.js` — one fictional warp ("Madder runner") partway through its
  cloth, with a handful of ends at different levels and conditions. A live
  *selvage line* reads the whole web at once: it runs straight and even
  when a few ends are raised and beaten, and puckers and waves as too many
  are raised at once, or left slack and untensioned. Keyboard-first, and it
  remembers your warp between visits.

## Running it

Open `index.html` in a browser. No build step.
