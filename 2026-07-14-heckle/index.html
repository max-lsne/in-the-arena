What comes off the brake, and what passes

The heap comes off the brake full of tow.
You draw a few of them to line.

Nothing comes off the flax-brake ready to spin. Every strick is a rough, matted handful — the long fibre and the short broken tow and the woody shive all tangled together. To heckle is to draw each one, by hand, through bed after bed of upright steel pins — coarse first, then fine — so the pins comb the tow out and split the long fibres down until they hang parallel and bright: line, the fibre you can actually spin. Draw it slow through each grade and the line comes off long. Force the whole heap through at once and you tear good line into tow.

Two readings, not one

A strick is read two ways at once.

How far it has been drawn is not the same question as how it is faring under the pins right now. A good dresser asks both of every handful on the bench.

Draw — how fine it is combed

  1. Ruffled — straight off the scutching knife: long, short, and shive all matted together. Nothing drawn yet.
  2. Coarse — pulled through the coarse heckle; the worst boon and the big tangles knocked out.
  3. Fine — drawn through the fine pins; the tow combed off, the long fibres splitting down.
  4. Line — dressed to line: long, parallel, lustrous fibre, ready for the wheel.

Condition — how it fares under the pins

  1. Tangled — matted with tow and shive; it snags and balks on the pins.
  2. Drawing — being pulled through; tow coming off in a soft grey fall.
  3. Combed — aligned and clean off the pins, but not yet struck to the light.
  4. Lustrous — dressed true and held to the light: it catches, and hangs like hair.

The whole of the method

Draw a few, slow. Let the rest wait in the heap.

  1. 1

    Set on only what you can draw

    The bench holds seven stricks and no more. When it is full the next handful waits in the heap — kept, not lost — until one is drawn to line and taken off.

  2. 2

    Draw grade by grade

    Move each strick coarse before fine, fine before line. Read it on both axes as you go: how far it is drawn, and how it is faring under the pins.

  3. 3

    Hold it to the light

    A strick is not done because it is combed. Strike it to the light. If it shines, it is line — take it off and set on the next. If it is dull, it goes back to the pins.

Who keeps one

For work that comes off rough and only passes when it shines.

The editor

Drafts come off the brake matted. Some you comb to line; most you stack. The bench is the few you are drawing bright, against the many left ruffled.

The maker

A prototype works is not a prototype dressed. Draw the few you mean to ship down to line, and hold each to the light before it goes.

The researcher

Findings come out tangled with tow. The bench is the few threads you are combing to a clean, parallel line — not the whole ruffled heap.
