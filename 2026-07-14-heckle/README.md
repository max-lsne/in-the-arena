# Heckle

**The whole scutched heap comes off the brake full of tow. You can only draw a
few of them to line — so draw those slow, and let the rest wait on the bench.**

Nothing comes off the flax-brake ready to spin. What you have after retting and
scutching is a rough, matted handful — a **strick** — still knotted with short
broken fibre and shive, the long stuff and the waste all tangled together. To
**heckle** is the flax-dresser's trade of drawing each strick, by hand, through
bed after bed of upright steel pins — coarse pins first, then finer — so the
pins comb the short tangled **tow** out of it and split the long fibres down
until what is left hangs parallel and lustrous: **line**, the fibre you can
actually spin. Draw it slow through each grade and the line comes off long and
bright; force the whole heap through the fine pins at once and you tear good
line into tow and pass a rough, ragged hank as dressed.

Heckle is a small page that holds that shape. It is a bench for the few stricks
you are drawing to line *properly* — combing slow, grade by grade — against the
many you scutched, stacked, and let sit with the tow still in.

> The word later drifted: to *heckle* a speaker is to comb them the same way,
> pin by pin. The bench is the older sense — patient, and about the fibre, not
> the sport.

## The two axes

A strick on the bench is read two ways at once, and they are not the same
reading.

**Draw** — how fine it has been combed:

- **Ruffled** — straight off the scutching knife: long and short and shive all
  matted together, nothing drawn yet.
- **Coarse** — pulled through the coarse heckle; the worst of the boon and the
  big tangles knocked out.
- **Fine** — drawn through the fine pins; the tow combed off, the long fibres
  splitting down and starting to lie parallel.
- **Line** — dressed to line: long, parallel, lustrous fibre, the tow all combed
  away, ready for the wheel.

**Condition** — how it is faring under the pins, right now:

- **Tangled** — matted with tow and shive; it snags and balks on the pins.
- **Drawing** — being pulled through; tow coming off into the pins in a soft grey
  fall.
- **Combed** — aligned and clean off the pins, but not yet struck to the light.
- **Lustrous** — dressed true and held to the light: it catches, and hangs like
  hair. Nothing left to draw.

The two come apart all the time. A strick can be well drawn to **Fine** and
still only **Combed** — every coarse tangle gone and the fibre lying parallel,
but nobody has yet held it to the light to see whether it shines. It can be
freshly **Ruffled** and already **Drawing**, because someone set on to comb it
before it was even sorted out of the heap. How far it is drawn and how it is
faring under the pins are different questions, and a good dresser asks both of
every strick on the bench.

## The bench holds seven

Heckling does not scale by wanting it to. Every strick on the bench wants the
same slow hand — drawn coarse, then fine, the tow combed clear, the line held to
the light. Crowd the bench and the hand hurries: stricks get forced through the
fine pins before the coarse work is done, long line gets torn into tow, and a
ragged hank passes for dressed because nobody held it up. So the bench has a
hard ceiling, and the ceiling is the point: **Heckle holds seven stricks.** When
the bench is full the next handful waits in the heap — kept, not lost — until one
has been drawn to line and taken off. A bench loaded past what one hand can draw
sends good fibre out as tow.

## The sheen

The whole bench is held to the light as one hank. A line across the top of the
board is that light drawn out: it lies **satin** — a slow, bright sheen — when
the work on the bench is drawing well and few stricks are worked at once, and it
**blooms dull and grey**, the way tow does, as the bench fills with ruffled,
half-combed stricks all balking on the pins at once. It is the fastest read
there is — you do not have to walk the bench to know it is drawing rough.

## The shape of the page

Hero, the problem stated plainly, three steps, and then the bench: a board of a
few seeded stricks you can set on, draw, move along the grades, rename, and reset
as you like — each read on both axes. No account, no cloud, no build step.
Everything you change is kept in your browser and nowhere else. Open
`index.html` in a browser; that is the whole of it.
