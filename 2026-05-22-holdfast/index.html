The grip is
the work.

Kelp does not have roots. It has a holdfast — a small, patient grip on the bedrock. The grip does not feed the kelp. It only keeps the wave from carrying it away. Holdfast is a small page for the few practices that do that for you. The ones that hold when the day fills up with everything else.

Habits
are for arrows.

Most habit apps want a streak. The streak is an arrow: a long unbroken line going up. The arrow does not know about the week the parent was sick, the month after the move, the morning the kettle was empty and you wrote the page anyway with a glass of water. The streak punishes you for the day you almost did not hold. The streak is the wrong shape.

A holdfast is the right shape. It does not grow up. It does not record a streak. It only grips the rock when the wave comes. Some of yours have gripped for years and you barely remember starting. Some are new and slip half the time. Some you have loosed and it was the right thing. The bed is the practice.

How it works

  1. 01

    Name a grip

    One small thing you mean to do most days. Small enough that you could do it on the worst day. Not a goal. A grip.

  2. 02

    Pick a tide

    Tide-in for the morning grip. Slack for the midday one. Tide-out for the grip that holds the night.

  3. 03

    Mark it when it grips

    One tap. No log entry. No mood. If it didn't grip today, it didn't. The bed is honest. So are you.

  4. 04

    Loose what slips

    If a grip cannot hold, take it off the rock. The bed reads truer when the loosed grips are loosed on purpose.

The three tides

  • Tide-in. The morning grip. The first thing, before the day fills up.
  • Slack. The midday grip. A small grip when the water sits still and the work is hardest.
  • Tide-out. The evening grip. The one that holds through the night and into tomorrow.

Who keeps a grip

The night nurse, two years in

Three tide-out grips and one tide-in. The tide-in is a cup of hot water before the curtain goes up. The streak app stopped working in week six of the rotation. The bed has held for ninety weeks.

The novelist on the third book

One slack grip: four hundred words before the post arrives. Some days the four hundred are bad and stay bad. The grip is not for good words. The grip is for sitting down.

The father of two under five

Loosed seven grips in a year. Kept three. The kept ones are: the kettle, the lane, the candle. The loosed ones were the right shape for a different season.

The dancer after the injury

Tide-in is twenty minutes on the floor — not the studio floor, the kitchen floor. Tide-out is the same twenty minutes. The middle of the day belongs to the physio. The bed reads like a tide chart.
