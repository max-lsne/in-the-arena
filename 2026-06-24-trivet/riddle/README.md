# Riddle

**A small page for shaking the heap down to the few that hold.**

You cannot keep every clod. The heap on the barrow is everything at once
— fine soil and grit and roots and stones, the dozen small asks and half
ideas all tipped in together. You shovel it onto the riddle and you shake.
The fine stuff finds the mesh and falls through in a haze, gone to the bed
below, and you let it go without a second look. What rides up to the top
is the few that wouldn't pass — the stone the spade keeps turning up, the
knot of root worth saving, the clod too good to lose. You knock those off
into the keep-pile, and the work that mattered is suddenly small enough to
hold. Riddle is a sieve for the many small commitments and ideas, shaken
down to the deliberate few that actually hold. (A riddle here is the
coarse garden and mason's sieve, not a puzzle.)

## The pitch in one breath

Keep the whole heap and you carry mud. Nothing on the barrow is bad — it
is all just soil and grit tipped together, and because you can't tell by
looking which clods will hold, you keep the lot and carry a load that is
mostly fine stuff that should have gone to the bed. The riddle is the
other way: you don't sort by staring, you put it on the mesh and shake,
and the sieve does the deciding. The fine falls through and is let go,
without guilt — it was always headed for the bed. What won't pass rides to
the top, and that is your answer: the deliberate few, clean of all the
loose stuff that was never going to hold. Shake the heap and keep what
won't fall through.

## The four stages

- **Heap** — straight off the spade, loose on the barrow, not yet on the
  mesh. The dozen maybes tipped in together, fine and coarse all at once.
  You can't tell what holds by looking; it has to be shaken first.
- **Riddled** — up on the wires and being worked, the fine soil starting
  to fall through. The ask you've begun to shake down rather than swallow
  whole. In the sieve at last, but nowhere near sorted.
- **Caught** — shaken a good while, riding up on top, plainly too big for
  the mesh. A commitment that has proven it won't pass — the loose stuff
  around it gone to the bed, the thing itself standing clear.
- **Kept** — knocked off into the keep-pile, the deliberate few you carry.
  The stone for the rockery, the root saved to replant, the one project
  that held. Riddled clean and worth the barrow.

## The four conditions

- **Loose** — just shovelled on, nothing shaken out yet, fine and coarse
  still all together. It has only this moment gone up on the mesh, and
  there is nothing to do but start to shake.
- **Shaking** — being worked side to side, the fine soil clouding down
  through the wire, the heap settling. Nothing to grade by hand — the
  riddle is letting fall what was always going to fall.
- **Holding** — the loose stuff mostly gone, the thing riding up and
  plainly too big for the mesh. It has shown it won't pass; what's left is
  to shake it clean and knock it off.
- **Cleared** — riddled through and tipped into the keep-pile, sorted and
  done. It is one of the deliberate few now. Some things ride up in a few
  shakes. A few want longer on the wire.

## What's in this folder

- `index.html` — the landing page, with a gardener's riddle scene: a
  round iron-rimmed sieve tilted over the barrow mid-shake, fine soil
  falling through the mesh in a haze to the pale bed below, stones and a
  knot of root riding up on top, a keep-pile of saved clods to the side,
  and a spade leaning blade-down in the turned bed.
- `styles.css` — a riddle palette: pale sieved-earth cream, the blued
  iron-green of the mesh and rim, garden-sun ochre, damp-loam brown, root
  and sward green, pale fresh tilth, gravel grey. Fraunces for the
  headings, Inter for the body, JetBrains Mono for the shakes.
- `script.js` — twelve things across a fictional plot in the middle of
  June 2026. The dozen "maybe" meetings shaken down to the two that hold,
  the stone the spade keeps turning up kept for the rockery, the seedling
  roots saved from the riddled compost, the clod that looked solid but
  broke and fell through after all.

## Running it

Open `index.html` in a browser. No build step.
