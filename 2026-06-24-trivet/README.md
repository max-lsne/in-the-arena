# Trivet

**Three small pages, made the same morning, for the same idea seen from three sides:
steadiness comes from a deliberate few, not an anxious many.**

Most days in this folder are one page. Some mornings the idea comes in
threes. A trivet is the little three-legged stand you set a hot pot on
over an uneven hearth — and it never rocks. A stool with four legs will
tip and wobble on a flagged floor, forever short on one corner; three
legs always find the stone, because three points are all a plane needs.
The lesson is older than the iron: you are steadier on a chosen few,
well placed, than on an anxious many. These three started apart — a
sharpening stone, a sundial, a garden sieve — and they meet here,
because they are all saying the one thing in three trades.

## The three

- **[Whetstone](./whetstone/)** — *keeping one edge keen beats hoarding
  dull blades.* A drawer of tools all goes dull at once from neglect; an
  edge is kept instead by a few strokes on the stone, often. A small
  page for the handful of skills you actually use and mean to keep
  cutting, against the many you only mean to own.
  Blunt → Ground → Honed → Stropped. Dull → Setting → Keening → Cutting.

- **[Gnomon](./gnomon/)** — *the day reads true off one fixed point, not
  a wall of clocks.* The gnomon is the shadow-caster that never moves;
  the whole day's hour is read off that one still thing while the sun
  travels. A small page for the single anchor your day can hang on —
  site one fixed habit and let the rest take its bearing from it.
  Loose → Sited → Trued → Fixed. Adrift → Setting → Holding → True.

- **[Riddle](./riddle/)** — *keep what won't fall through, let the fine
  stuff go.* A riddle is the coarse garden sieve: you shake the heap and
  keep only the stones and roots that won't pass, and let the rest fall
  away without grieving it. A small page for the many small asks shaken
  down to the deliberate few that actually hold.
  Heap → Riddled → Caught → Kept. Loose → Shaking → Holding → Cleared.

## The shared shape

Each page is the same small machine the folder keeps building: a hero,
the problem stated plainly, four steps, an interactive board of a dozen
seeded entries you can sort, advance, and reset, four people who keep
one of these for real, and a quiet waitlist that promises one email and
no streaks. Four levels, four conditions, a number that only goes up if
you keep coming back. No build step — open any `index.html` in a browser.
