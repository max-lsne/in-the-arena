# Gnomon

**A small page for siting the one fixed thing your day reads off.**

A sundial has no moving parts. The whole travelling day — the long
shadow of morning, the short hard mark of noon, the slant gold of
evening — is read off one thing that never moves: the gnomon, the fixed
style set true to the pole, throwing its edge across the cut hour-lines.
The sun does all the going; the gnomon does all the telling. You do not
build the day out of a dozen wobbling routines and hope they keep step.
You site one fixed point — the early desk, the noon walk, the evening
shutdown — set it true, leave it unmoving, and let everything else take
its bearing from where its shadow falls. Gnomon is a place to keep that
one anchor, and to watch it harden over the days from a loose idea into
a fixture the whole day orients to.

## The pitch in one breath

A wall of clocks tells no hour — set them all going at once and they
drift and disagree, and you spend the day correcting them instead of
working. Structure scattered across many small routines is not
structure; it is a dozen more things to keep wound. The gnomon is the
other thing. Site one still point, set it true, and don't move it to
suit the day. Then the rest need not be timed at all — it reads off the
one unmoving mark, things landing before it or after it, in its shadow
or in the light. Steadiness comes from a deliberate few, not an anxious
many: one anchor, held true, reads the whole travelling day.

## The four marks

- **Loose** — just chosen, not yet set to anything. The point you mean
  to anchor the day to but haven't pinned down — a line written on the
  lid. It will drift off the dial the first busy morning unless sited.
- **Sited** — set to a real hour and a real place, the foot found, the
  style standing. Committed to and beginning to cast a shadow you can
  read — but newly set, and not yet proven against a hard day.
- **Trued** — held over a season and squared to the pole, the shadow
  falling clean on the hour-lines. A point kept long enough that the day
  has started, on its own, to orient to it.
- **Fixed** — mortared in, held true for years, the unmoving thing the
  whole day reads off. The early desk kept a decade, the noon walk that
  never fails. So set you no longer think to question it.

## The four conditions

- **Adrift** — newly chosen and apt to wander, set to no hour and no
  place yet. It has only just been picked, and there is nothing holding
  it where you want it. A busy morning and it is gone.
- **Setting** — finding its hour and its place, the foot beginning to
  bite. Nothing to force — it is taking the few days it needs before it
  will stand on its own against an ordinary day.
- **Holding** — kept true over a season, the shadow starting to fall
  clean. The steadying is happening, day on day, underneath. The day is
  beginning to read off it without being told to.
- **True** — held true and set, a fixture, the mark the whole day reads
  off. It will keep for years. Some points come good in a season; a few
  want longer before they stop needing a thought.

## What's in this folder

- `index.html` — the landing page, with a keeper's dial scene: a round
  weathered stone face on a tooled plinth in a churchyard, the cut
  hour-lines radiating out, roman numerals bleached round the rim, the
  noon meridian deep-cut down the middle, and the one angled bronze
  gnomon throwing its hard shadow across the face while the low evening
  sun does all the travelling.
- `styles.css` — a gnomon palette: sun-bleached limestone cream,
  weathered antique bronze, brass highlight, aged tawny brass, the deep
  blue-grey of the cast shadow, pale and full verdigris, weathered stone
  grey, chalk-white noon. Fraunces for the headings, Inter for the body,
  JetBrains Mono for the days held.
- `script.js` — twelve anchor points across a fictional keeper's day in
  the middle of June 2026. The 6am desk held before the house wakes, the
  noon walk the whole afternoon hangs on, the meridian line cut in the
  old churchyard dial, the evening shutdown still adrift and set only
  last week, the midnight scroll that was let drift and dropped off the
  dial at Whitsun.

## Running it

Open `index.html` in a browser. No build step.
