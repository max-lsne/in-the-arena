# Whetstone

**A small page for keeping the few tools you use keen.**

A blade left in a drawer goes dull from neglect. You don't keep an edge
by owning more tools; you keep it by a few strokes on the stone, often,
before the work. A wet stone, a little oil, the blade drawn flat over
the grit a dozen unhurried passes, the wire wiped off on the strop — a
minute, most days. The edge never goes off because it is never left
long enough to. Whetstone is a maker's rack for the few skills and
tools you actually use, kept sharp by small frequent practice instead
of accumulated by the dozen and left to rust.

## The pitch in one breath

A drawer of dull blades cuts nothing; one tool kept keen cuts all day.
The anxious answer to needing an edge is to buy another blade and grind
it bright once — and by spring you have ten dull knives, the unopened
app, the instrument in the corner, the framework you bookmarked. Steel
does not wait; left down it rolls and rusts, and the skill used once and
shelved is just as gone. The stone is the other answer: keep few, take
them to the grit a little and often, and sharpness is simply where they
live. You cannot stockpile sharpness. You can only keep it.

## The four edges

- **Blunt** — bought, owned, and never yet touched to the stone. The
  new knife in its box, the app installed and unopened, the skill you
  mean to start. Capability on paper, dull in the hand.
- **Ground** — taken to the stone a first few times, the rough edge
  knocked off, the bevel set. A real start — but a new edge, still soft,
  that will roll the moment you neglect it.
- **Honed** — worked on the stone over weeks until it bites true. The
  skill you practise a little most days, coming good and reliable. It
  cuts cleanly and holds, because you keep coming back to the grit.
- **Stropped** — kept razor by a stroke a day for years. The edge that
  parts a hair, that the work never fights. The deep tool that is sharp
  not because you ground it hard, but because you never let it dull.

## The four conditions

- **Dull** — never honed, or long since gone off. The edge will not cut;
  there is nothing for it but to take it to the stone and begin.
- **Setting** — the first strokes are setting the bevel, knocking the
  roughness off, finding the angle. The edge is being made, soft and new
  and needing to be kept up before it rolls back.
- **Keening** — the slow keening change, the bite coming on, stroke over
  stroke across the weeks. The sharpness that was impossible in the
  blunt is coming now, a little and often, on its own steady count.
- **Cutting** — keen and true and held that way, ready for the work. It
  parts the wood by its own weight. Some edges come good in a fortnight.
  A few are kept razor for years by a stroke a day.

## What's in this folder

- `index.html` — the landing page, with a maker's bench scene: a dished
  oil stone in its wooden cradle, the oil sheen and dark swarf across
  its face, a blade laid flat and drawn over the grit mid-stroke, a
  struck spark where steel meets stone, a leather strop hanging on the
  wall, finished blades resting at the back, a little oil bottle by the
  cradle, low work-lamp light from above.
- `styles.css` — a steel palette: pale oiled-stone grey, slate steel
  blue-grey, struck-spark amber, worn-brass bronze, swarf near-black,
  bright-steel pale, water-stone blue-grey, honing-stone grey. Fraunces
  for the headings, Inter for the body, JetBrains Mono for the strokes.
- `script.js` — twelve edges across a fictional maker's rack in the
  middle of June 2026. The chef's knife honed every morning since 2011,
  the second language kept up ten minutes a day, the bench chisel honed
  before every mortise for forty years, the half-learned guitar gone to
  rust in its case under the bed.

## Running it

Open `index.html` in a browser. No build step.
