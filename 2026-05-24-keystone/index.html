Task lists
are not arches.

A list is a heap. Forty things, all the same shape, all asking the same way. At the end of the week the ones you did look like the ones you didn't. The list does not know which one was the keystone. It does not even know there was one. Most weeks there is one.

An arch is a shape. The keystone goes in last but is named first — the whole arch is built to receive it. The voussoirs lean against it. The springers begin the curve. When the keystone sets, the arch stands, and the rest of the stones are honest about what they were doing the whole time.

How it works

  1. 01

    Name the keystone

    One task this week. The one that, if it lands, makes the week the week. Name it before you name anything else.

  2. 02

    Cut the voussoirs

    The supporting stones. A few — five, maybe seven. Each one leans against the keystone or against another voussoir.

  3. 03

    Set them as they hold

    One tap. Cut becomes Set when a stone is in place. Set becomes Placed when the mortar is dry. The arch redraws.

  4. 04

    Remove what does not fit

    If a stone cannot hold the curve, pull it. The arch reads truer when the removed stones are removed on purpose.

The three positions

  • Keystone. The one stone the week is built around. Only one. Set last.
  • Voussoir. A supporting stone. Leans against the keystone or against another voussoir.
  • Springer. A stone at the foot of the arch. Small. The thing that lets the week start standing.

Who sets a keystone

The engineering lead in a quiet quarter

One keystone a week, every week of the quarter. Five quarters in. The keystones are not the biggest tickets — they are the ones the team will read in the changelog and feel the room shift on a Tuesday.

The thesis writer in year four

The keystone is a page or a paragraph. Some weeks it is a sentence. The voussoirs are reading and pacing the hallway. The springer is opening the file at the same time of day.

The founder before the raise

Six weeks of the same keystone: a story that fits in a single breath. The voussoirs were everything else — the model, the deck, the back-channel — and they all leaned against the breath.

The teacher in the last month of term

The keystone is the one lesson the class will remember in July. The voussoirs are the four lessons that prepare it. The arch closes the week before the long break.
