cardan · noun · /ˈkɑːdən/ — the cross-and-yoke joint that lets a turn pass between two shafts set at an angle, after Gerolamo Cardano, who described it in 1545; also a universal joint, or Hooke's joint, for the English scientist who gave its geometry

Angle

A turn can be carried around a corner. It cannot be carried around a corner evenly.

A universal joint ties two shafts that do not line up. Each shaft ends in a forked yoke; between them sits a cross — a four-armed spider, one pair of arms held by each yoke. Turn the first shaft and the cross tumbles, and the second shaft is dragged round with it. The two shafts meet at a bend angle β, and across a whole revolution the output turns exactly once for each turn of the input — nothing is lost. But it does not keep step within the turn. As one yoke tips toward the plane of the bend its grip on the cross shortens, and the far shaft is hurried along; a quarter-turn later it is held back. The output angle is arctan(tan θ / cos β) — an S-shaped lead and lag about the straight line, agreeing four times a turn, adrift between. Flatten the bend and the wobble shrinks to nothing; open it, and it grows.

Why the corner costs evenness

The one relation, read for its wobble

  • Project the driving yoke's arm onto the driven shaft's plane and the geometry falls out: tan φ = tan θ / cos β. Since cos β ≤ 1, the output tangent is always stretched — but the stretch bites unevenly, and the net over a turn is exactly one revolution for one.
  • At θ = 0, 90, 180, 270° the formula gives φ = θ — the quarter points, where input and output agree. These are the only four angles at which they are truly in step.
  • Between them the output leads through one quarter and lags through the next, by up to roughly β²/4 for small bends — about 4° at a 30° bend, near 11° at 45°. The error is a wobble, not a drift: it always comes back.
  • Flatten the bend and the cross barely tumbles — cos β → 1, φ → θ, the joint vanishes into a plain coupling. Open the bend toward 90° and the yoke's grip collapses: the relation breaks down, which is why a real joint is kept well short of a right angle.
  • The uneven angle is only the half of it. Differentiate, and the uneven speed appears — a flutter twice a turn, the subject of ripple; cancel it with a second joint, and you have the shaft under every car — double.

The joint keeps its promise over the whole turn and breaks it at every instant inside — one revolution out for one in, but never the same revolution at the same moment.
