# Tarn

**A small page for the high water that stays through the dry summer.**

A tarn is a small mountain lake, usually sitting in the bowl a glacier
left ten thousand years ago — a hollow cupped in scree and sedge,
fed by snowmelt above and a slow seep through the moss, drained by
a thin beck that runs out over the lip. Below the treeline they are
called pools or pots. Above, where nothing taller than a juniper
will hold, they are tarns. In a dry August the lower pools may go;
the high tarns stay. By the time the swifts have left in September
the water is dark enough to lose a stone in.

## The pitch in one breath

Most of the year's water comes and goes — the becks rise after rain
and are dry in two days, the puddles in the lane last a morning.
The tarn is the other thing. The tarn is what holds. Snow falls on
the fell in November, melts into the bowl in May, and is still
there in August when every roadside ditch is bone-dry stone. A tarn
is what a hill keeps for itself when no one is asking.

## The four levels

- **Drop** — a single drop of meltwater off the rock above. The
  first half-page, the first morning of the practice, the line
  that is the only line. Not yet a pool. Could evaporate by noon.
- **Pool** — a small gathered pool, days deep, summer-fragile.
  The first finished draft, the first ten weeks of the practice
  carried unbroken. Real, but the next dry August might lose it.
- **Tarn** — a proper mountain lake, year-round, twenty feet deep
  in the middle. A body of work the same hands have built up
  patiently over years. Will not blow away in a single bad season.
- **Cirque** — the full glacial bowl with three tarns and a string
  of feeder pools and the beck running out under the col. A
  lifework, a landscape, a watershed visible from the next fell.

## The four conditions

- **Glassed** — perfectly still, no wind on the surface, the whole
  fell upside down in it. The work is held, settled, quiet.
- **Riffled** — wind on the water, the surface alive with the
  weather. The work is being done in small visible movements.
- **Spawning** — the trout are running up the inlet beck, the
  duckweed shifting, the otter at the far edge at dawn. The work
  is alive underneath, populated, busy with its own creatures.
- **Frozen** — winter-locked, the surface thick enough to walk on
  by the shoreline. The work is held but not moving. Some tarns
  thaw in May. A few never quite do.

## What's in this folder

- `index.html` — the landing page, with a small high tarn cupped in
  scree and sedge under a long ridge, a thin beck running out under
  the col, a pair of mergansers on the far water.
- `styles.css` — a tarn palette: slate, water, shallow, glacier,
  sedge, stone, moss, snow, shadow, ink. Fraunces for the
  headings, Inter for the body, JetBrains Mono for the depths.
- `script.js` — twelve tarns and pools mapped across a fictional
  fellside parish in the middle of June 2026. The cathedral tarn at
  the head of the cirque since the 1872 survey, the young walker's
  first drop in the upper bowl this snowmelt, the lost tarn in the
  dry valley that has been gone since 2019.

## Running it

Open `index.html` in a browser. No build step.
