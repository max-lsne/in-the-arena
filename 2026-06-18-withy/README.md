# Withy

**A small page for the slow work you grow a year and weave for a lifetime.**

A withy is a willow rod the wet moor throws up in a single year,
straight as a wand off a pollarded stool. Below the workshop it is
just willow; on the bench, cut and soaked and ready, it is a withy.
You cut it in the dead of winter, when the sap is down and the leaf
gone, tie it into bolts, and sink them in the soak-pit for three
weeks of tea-brown water until the rod bends round your wrist without
a crack in it. Only then will it weave. A basket of green, hurried
withy splits at the first bend; one of seasoned rod carries a
hundredweight for forty years.

## The pitch in one breath

Most work is green and forced — cut in the morning and woven in the
afternoon, with the sap still up in it, and it cracks the first time
you ask it to take a real bend. A withy is the other thing. A withy
is given its year on the moor, its cut in the dead of winter, its
three weeks under the soak. You cannot hurry it. You give it the
winter and the soak, and then it will weave.

## The four levels

- **Rod** — a single year's willow rod, just cut, the sap still in
  it. The first half-page, the empty notebook with one line, the
  morning you began. Green; it will crack if you force the bend today.
- **Bolt** — a tied bundle, sorted to length, weeks in the soak. The
  first real gathering of work, supple now and ready to go in. Real,
  but not yet woven into anything that holds.
- **Basket** — a finished weave, a creel or a hurdle or an eel-trap
  that holds. A body of work woven tight enough to carry a
  hundredweight, by hands that gave every rod its soak.
- **Holt** — the osier holt itself, the standing ground that throws
  new rods every winter for a lifetime. A lifework, a craft, the bed
  your grandfather planted and you still cut.

## The four conditions

- **Green** — fresh-cut, the sap still in it, brittle. It will not
  bend yet; force it now and it splits. The work has only just begun.
- **Soaking** — sunk in the pit, days under the tea-brown water,
  coming supple. Nothing to watch, nothing to hurry — the rod is
  taking the water it needs.
- **Weaving** — on the bench, the rods going in, the weave growing.
  The randing and the slewing that were impossible in the green are
  easy now. The work is being made.
- **Seasoned** — dried and set, the buff withy hard, the weave
  holding. It will carry a hundredweight for forty years. Some rods
  come good in a season. A few want longer.

## What's in this folder

- `index.html` — the landing page, with a withy-bed on the flat wet
  moor: a row of pollarded stools throwing up fans of straight rods,
  a soak-pit with a tied bolt leaning out of the dark water, a
  half-woven basket base in the near corner.
- `styles.css` — a withy palette: ground-mist, brown withy, buff,
  russet basket, the dark holt, pale green rod, osier, moss, stone,
  white-withy cream. Fraunces for the headings, Inter for the body,
  JetBrains Mono for the winters.
- `script.js` — twelve rods, bolts, baskets and the holt itself
  across a fictional withy-grower's moor on the Somerset Levels in
  the middle of January 2026. The grandfather's holt cut since the
  1940s, the young hand's first green rod cut this midwinter, the
  cracked creel that went to the bonfire last Candlemas.

## Running it

Open `index.html` in a browser. No build step.
