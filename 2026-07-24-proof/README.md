# Proof

**Three small pages, made the same morning, for the same idea seen from three
trades: you learn what a thing is by testing a small, faithful sample of it
first — and never by trusting the mark it wears or committing the whole and
finding out at the end.**

Most days in this folder are one page. Some mornings the idea arrives already in
threes. *Proof* is the word three trades reach for when the making is not the
making but the *checking*: the thing is finished — a batch mixed, a forme set, a
bar cast and stamped — and now, before it is trusted with a real commitment, it
has to be **proved**. Not admired, not weighed by eye, not taken on its stamp:
proved, by a small sample that runs the same risk the whole runs and reports the
truth while the mistake is still cheap.

Three trades prove a thing they each mean a little differently, and each learned
the same lesson the hard way. The baker sets a **sponge** — a spoonful of the
batch, put by warm overnight — and reads in the morning whether the culture is
alive, because a dead culture found on a spoonful costs a spoonful, and the same
culture found in the trough costs the morning. The printer pulls a **galley**
proof — one inked sheet off the locked forme — and reads it cold for the turned
letter, because a typo caught on the proof costs one sheet, and the same typo
caught after the run costs the whole edition. The assayer cuts an **assay** — a
draw of a few grains tried against a known standard — and learns what the metal
actually is, because a debased bar caught on a draw costs a few grains, and the
same bar bought on its stamp costs the price of fine for base.

They start in different shops — a bakehouse, a composing room, an assay office —
and they meet here, because they are all the one habit said in three trades:
**prove a small, faithful part before you commit the whole; read what the test
says, not what the mark promises.**

## The three

- **[Sponge](./sponge/)** — *test what you can lose before you commit what you
  can't.* A dead culture looks and smells fine right up until the dough will not
  rise, hours in, with the batch mixed and the oven hot. Set a spoonful apart the
  night before and read whether it domes; the flat one caught on a spoonful never
  costs you the sack. A small page for the small, faithful trial you run ahead of
  a commitment you cannot take back.
  Set → Waking → Domed / Flat.

- **[Galley](./galley/)** — *read the cheap copy so you never reprint the dear
  one.* The press prints what is there a thousand times with equal care; it
  catches nothing. Pull one sheet, read it cold as a stranger to the copy, and
  the turned letter costs five minutes with a pencil instead of the whole
  edition. A small page for reading the throwaway copy closely before you
  multiply the one you can't recall.
  Glanced → Skimmed → Read cold / Sent unread.

- **[Assay](./assay/)** — *value the metal on the test, not on the stamp.* A mark
  is only as honest as whoever struck it, and metal can be debased in ways no eye
  or scale will find. Cut a draw of a few grains, try it against a known
  standard, and price the bar on what it is rather than what it claims. A small
  page for testing a real sample of the thing before you trust the claim on its
  face.
  Eyed → Weighed → Touched → Cupelled.

## The shared shape

Each page is the same small machine the folder keeps building: a hero, the
problem stated plainly, four rungs from the laziest check to the one that pays,
three steps, and then an interactive board of a dozen seeded entries. On every
board the same choice repeats a dozen times — spend a little to **prove** the
sample and learn the truth, or **commit** it blind and stake the whole on a
guess — and a live gauge keeps the tally honest: how many you proved, how many
faults you caught cheap, and how many you let ride on trust. Reseed the board and
the odds wander, so no two runs read the same. No build step — open any
`index.html` in a browser. It all lives in your browser; nothing leaves it.
