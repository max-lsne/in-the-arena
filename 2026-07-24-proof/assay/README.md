# Assay

**You do not value the lot on the stamp it wears. You value it on a draw you
tested yourself.**

An **assay** is the test a metal must pass before anyone trusts it at its claimed
fineness — a draw of a few grains, cut, weighed, and tried against a known
standard, that reports what the bar actually is rather than what its stamp says
it is. The stamp is a promise; the assay is the proof. Metal can be debased in
ways no eye or scale will find — alloyed down, plated over, cored with something
base — and the mark travels with the bar asserting a fineness the metal may not
hold. A few grains, spent on a test, stand between you and paying fine for a
whole lot of base.

Assay is a small page built around that one move: test a real sample of the thing
before you trust the claim on its face.

> A mark is only as honest as whoever struck it. The stamp is the surface the
> seller chose to show you. The assay cuts through it — and it is the one thing a
> false stamp cannot answer.

## The lot, and the draw

- **The lot — valued on the claim.** The whole parcel, every bar priced at the
  fineness its stamp asserts. Trust the marks and you have bought a promise by
  weight. If one bar is cored with base metal, you paid fine for it and find out
  only when you go to sell or to melt.
- **The draw — valued on the test.** A few grains cut from the bar and actually
  tried. It costs a sliver of metal and a little time, and it returns the one
  number the stamp cannot fake: what the bar is, not what it says.

## What the touch tells

There are cheaper tests than a true assay, and each can be fooled by a bar built
to pass it. Knowing which test you ran is half of knowing what you know:

- **Eyed — it looks right.** Colour is the first thing a plater gets right; a
  base bar washed in gold passes the eye every time.
- **Weighed — the mass is close.** Density is hard to fake, but a core of the
  right heavy base metal brings the weight back into range. A scale narrows the
  field; it does not close it.
- **Touched — a streak on the stone.** A real assay in miniature. It reads the
  surface truly and catches most debasement; a deep plating can still lie
  beneath it.
- **Cupelled — a draw tried to the metal.** The settling test. A sample cut
  through the bar reports the fineness of the metal itself, core and all. It
  costs the draw; it cannot be shown a false face.

The assay stands for any test that samples the real thing rather than its
presentation — the reference check, the audited figure, the traced claim. Run the
test that matches the price you are about to pay.

## The assay bench

The bench lays out a dozen bars, each wearing a fineness stamp. Some are true to
their mark and some are debased beneath it, and every one looks the part.
**Assay** a bar to cut a draw and learn what it really is — it comes back true or
light of its stamp — or **value** it blind and pay the price the mark asks. The
gauge keeps the tally: bars assayed, debased bars caught on a few grains, and
bars valued on the stamp alone. *Fresh lot* re-sets the bench, and the number of
debased bars — and how light they run — wanders each time, so no two lots read
the same.
