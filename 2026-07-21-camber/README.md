# Camber

**You do not build a beam to the shape you want it to hold. You build it to the
shape that becomes true once the load arrives — proud by exactly what the load
will take away.**

A **camber** is the slight upward curve a maker builds into a beam, a deck, or a
road on purpose. Sighted down the edge before it is ever loaded, it looks like a
mistake — a hump where you wanted a straight line. It is the opposite of a
mistake. Every span settles under what it carries; build it dead level and the
only way left to go is down, into a visible dip. So you build it *proud* by the
amount the load will pull it back, and the load itself brings it true. The good
camber is the one you cannot see once the beam is carrying.

Camber is a small page built around that one move. It is a bench for a single
beam: set the span and the load — the two things you do not control — read the
deflection they fix between them, and build in a crown to meet it.

> A beam built dead level is the classic way to end up with a sagging one. The
> level was measured; the load was not. The deflection is what you did not build
> in, and it always collects below the line.

## Deflection, or camber

Two things decide where a loaded beam comes to rest, and the maker sets only one
of them before the load is ever there:

- **Deflection — what the load takes.** How far a loaded beam drops below where
  it started, fixed by the span and the weight. You cannot argue it away; a long
  beam under a real load *will* come down. The only choice is where it comes down
  from.
- **Camber — what you build in.** The upward crown you put in on purpose, before
  the load, so there is somewhere for the beam to settle down *to*. Match it to
  the deflection and the beam lands on the line — invisible when it works,
  glaring when it does not.

## How much to build in

The crown is a judgement set by the span and the load, not by taste. Too little
and it dips; too much and it humps. Coarsest error on top, the mark you want in
the middle:

- **No camber — dead flat as built.** The honest-looking choice, and the wrong
  one. The load settles below the line into a dip you see from across the room.
- **Shy — less than the deflection.** A crown, but not enough of one; the load
  eats it and keeps going. Smaller dip, still a dip.
- **True — camber equals deflection.** The target. Built proud by exactly what
  the load will take, brought level by the load. You cannot see it because it
  worked.
- **Proud — more than the deflection.** Over-built. The load can't pull it all
  the way down; the beam keeps a hump you feel underfoot or see against the sky.
- **Way proud — a permanent arch.** The camber you build when you fear the sag
  and never measure it. It never settles; it just stands there, curved.

The crown stands for any correction you build in ahead of a load you know is
coming — the buffer, the head start, the over-provision. Aim it at the
deflection you can actually expect. Build for a sag that never comes and you are
left with a hump.

## The bench holds one beam

The bench draws a single beam across two piers against a level datum. Set the
span and the load and it works out the deflection — how far this beam will drop —
and draws the ghost of where a flat beam would end up, below the line. That
number is your target. Build in the camber to meet it: short of it the beam dips,
past it the beam humps, and in the narrow band where the crown and the deflection
cancel the reading comes **true** and the loaded beam lands on the datum. *Build
it true* sets the crown to the deflection in one move; the presets hand you a
shelf, a mantel, a joist, a footbridge, and a barn ridge to camber for yourself.
The beam you lay out — its name, span, load, and crown — is kept in your browser
and nowhere else, so it is still there, set as you left it, when you come back.

## The shape of the page

Hero, the two shapes a beam has (built and arrived-at), the ladder from flat to
over-arched, three steps, and then the bench: a beam, a span and a load that fix
the deflection, a camber you build in, and a reading that comes true where the
two cancel. Set the span, set the load, build the crown, and land it on the line.
The whole bench is keyboard-workable — <kbd>[</kbd>/<kbd>]</kbd> to shape the
crown, <kbd>,</kbd>/<kbd>.</kbd> for the load, <kbd>−</kbd>/<kbd>=</kbd> for the
span, <kbd>T</kbd> to build it true, and <kbd>R</kbd> to reset — and every change
is read out to screen readers through a polite status line. No account, no cloud,
no build step. Open `index.html` in a browser; that is the whole of it.
