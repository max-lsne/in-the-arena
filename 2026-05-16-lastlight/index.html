You are leaving in October. Or July. Or the second week of a month you can already see. The boxes will get packed. The forwarding will get filed. The harder part is the bench, the counter, the corner where the river bends — the small things you'll only do here. Lastlight keeps that list while there's still light to do them in.

Moving is logistics.
Leaving is a list.

The packing app is for boxes. The to-do app is for cancellations. Nothing is for the long walk along the canal, or the cafe where the owner already knows your order, or the friend who's been "soon, soon, soon" for two years.

Those are the things you'll miss. They aren't tasks. They're a goodbye, written one line at a time. Lastlight is built to hold that, and only that — for as many days as you have left.

How it works

  1. 01

    Set a leave date

    The day you'll sleep somewhere else for good. Lastlight counts down only this. It will not nag. It will not buzz.

  2. 02

    Add small things

    A walk. A table. A person. An errand you kept meaning to run. A quiet place. Add five, or fifty. There is no right number.

  3. 03

    Tend the list

    When you do one, mark it kept. When you decide not to, mark it passed. Passing is part of leaving. The list narrows.

  4. 04

    Take the trail with you

    On the day, the list closes. You get a small, plain page — what you kept, what you passed, the shape of the last weeks. That's it.

The five kinds of light

  • Walk. A route, a view, a stretch of river. Somewhere your feet already know.
  • Table. A meal, a coffee, a counter where someone says your name.
  • People. One person, one short visit, before the city moves someone out of reach.
  • Errand. The small place you kept meaning to try, and now you have the excuse.
  • Quiet. A bench, a stairwell, a roof. Somewhere to just sit and notice it once more.

Who keeps a lastlight

The renter on a 60-day notice

The lease ends in October. Most of the small places on your list are within a mile of the apartment you're about to give back. Lastlight is the only thing that knows that.

The student finishing a year abroad

Six weeks left. Final papers are in the calendar. The cafe near the library, the train station bench, the river on Sunday morning — those aren't.

The friend who got the job

You said yes. The boxes are coming. Somewhere between the visa and the lease, the smaller list — the bookshop, the long walk, dinner with the people who watched you decide — needs its own page.

The grandparent's last summer

They're closing the house. One season left. The list is for them, and partly for you, and entirely for the small things you both want to do once more before the rooms go quiet.
