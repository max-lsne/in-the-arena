# Towpath

**A small page for the slow steady haul.**

A towpath is the worn ribbon of stone beside the canal. A horse walks
it at three miles an hour. A rope runs from its collar down to the bow
of a narrowboat carrying forty tons of coal. The horse is not strong
enough to lift the boat. The horse is barely strong enough to lift
itself. But the rope is taut, and the boat moves, and at the end of
the day the boat is twelve miles further along than it was at dawn.
That is the whole thing.

## The pitch in one breath

Most productivity asks what your top speed is. The towpath asks what
your slowest sustainable speed is — the pace you could keep up for a
year without stopping. A narrowboat at full sprint still loses to a
narrowboat under steady tow. The current does not help and does not
hurt; the rope only has to stay taut. The towpath is the practice of
not letting it go slack.

## The four loads

- **Skiff** — the small light work that sits high in the water: a
  daily note, a single push-up, a short letter. Easy to pull. Easy to
  forget you are pulling.
- **Wherry** — the small working work: a chapter to be drafted, a
  garden corner to be cleared, an instrument to be practised a half
  hour at a time. Moves at three miles an hour without fuss.
- **Narrowboat** — the long-haul work that takes a year or two: a
  novel, a thesis, a slow recovery, a course finished evening by
  evening. Will not move without the rope. Will not stop once it has.
- **Barge** — the heaviest, slowest, longest hauls: the decade-long
  works, the rebuilt house, the second career. The pace is the same
  three miles an hour. The barge does not know it is a barge.

## The four states of a haul

- **Cast off** — the lines are loose, the boat is drifting from the
  wharf. Not yet under way.
- **In tow** — the rope is taut, the horse is walking. The boat is
  moving but has not yet found its rhythm in the water.
- **Steady haul** — the long middle. Mile after mile of the same pace.
  No story here, which is the point.
- **Made fast** — arrived at the next pound or the final wharf. Tied
  up at a bollard. The haul has done what it was going to do.

## What's in this folder

- `index.html` — the landing page, with a small canal and a small
  horse pulling a small boat.
- `styles.css` — a towpath palette: limestone, mist, canal-water,
  canal-deep, moss, hemp, copper-glint, barge-wood, shadow, ink;
  Fraunces for headings, Inter for body, JetBrains Mono for the bollard
  marks.
- `script.js` — twelve seeded loads kept by no one in particular along
  a slow turn of the spring of 2026. A translation chapter by chapter,
  a garden rebuilt over five seasons, a marathon prepared for in single
  mile additions, a novel begun in 2014 now on its second draft.
