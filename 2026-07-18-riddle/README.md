# Riddle

**You cannot sort a heap by everything at once. Tip it onto a screen, pick one
gauge, shake — and let the mesh do the sorting.**

A **riddle** is a coarse sieve: a hooped screen of mesh you tip a heap of mixed
stuff onto and shake. You do not weigh each stone and rule on it by hand. The
mesh rules, and it asks one question only — *are you smaller than this gap?* What
is fine enough falls **through** to the pan; what is too big stays **over** on
the mesh. That is the whole of its cleverness: it sorts by **one gauge**, and
sorting by one gauge is exactly what makes a hopeless heap fall into a clean
gradient in a few shakes. Try to sort by ten questions at once and you are back
to picking the pile over stone by stone.

Riddle is a small page built around that discipline. It is a screen for a heap of
work too big to pick apart by hand — a backlog, an inbox, a quarter's worth of
maybes. You tip it all in, gauge each piece by a single question, and shake it
down into bands from boulder to dust.

> A garden riddle and a laboratory sieve stack are the same idea at two scales:
> one gap, one reading, and the pile arranges itself. The skill is never in the
> mesh. It is in choosing which single question to riddle by first.

## Through, or over

A riddle has no opinion about worth. It has a gap, and it reads every piece
against that one gap. Everything lands in one of two places:

- **Through — the fines.** Small enough to fall clean through into the pan. The
  quick, light pieces a single gauge settles for good. Do them on the spot, or
  let them go.
- **Over — the tailings.** Too big to pass. They stay up on the mesh — and that
  is not a verdict of importance, only of size against *this* gauge. The tailings
  are what you riddle again, through a finer screen and a different question.

## The ladder

Stack the screens coarsest on top, finest at the bottom, a solid pan beneath.
Shake, and every piece settles on the first screen too fine for it to pass. One
shake, one gauge, and the heap lies in bands:

- **Stone** — won't pass any screen. The project you keep meaning to start.
- **Cobble** — a week's weight; real work that needs planning before it moves.
- **Gravel** — an afternoon's worth; a task with edges you can already see.
- **Grit** — small and quick, but not nothing; caught only on the finest mesh.
- **Dust** — the two-minute thing. Falls clean through to the pan. Do it as it
  drops, or blow it away.

Size here stands for whatever single gauge you choose — bulk, cost, urgency,
risk. The riddle does not care which. It asks only that you pick **one**, and it
will spread the heap along it.

## The screen holds ten

A riddle loaded past its size just blinds itself with dust and sorts nothing, so
the screen has a hard ceiling: **ten pieces at a time.** When it is full, take
some fines off or clear a band before you tip more in. Screen less at once and
you actually see the sort; screen the whole tip in one go and you see only dust.

## The dust

Shaking a heap raises dust, and the dust is the fastest read on the page. A band
across the top of the screen hangs **thick and grey** while pieces sit unshaken
in the hopper, and settles **clear** as the heap grades out and comes to rest.
You do not have to count the hopper to know there is unsorted work on the screen
— the air over it tells you.

## The shape of the page

Hero, the two places a piece can land, the ladder, three steps, and then the
screen itself: a hopper you tip pieces into, a graded stack of bands, and a shake
that sends the heap down into them. Add pieces, gauge each by size, shake, tip a
band back to riddle it again. No account, no cloud, no build step. What you tip
in and how it grades out is kept in your browser and nowhere else — the screen is
still there, sorted as you left it, when you come back. Nothing is sent anywhere.
Open `index.html` in a browser; that is the whole of it.
