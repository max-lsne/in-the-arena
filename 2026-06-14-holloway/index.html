A line walked into the ground

A field has no path.
A holloway is the path you walked.

A holloway is a sunken way — a lane cut down into the soft sandstone or chalk of the countryside by the slow, repeated passage of feet, hooves, and cartwheels over centuries. No one decides to dig a holloway. It is not a road that was built. It is a road that was walked — and walked, and walked, until the ground gave way underfoot and the path settled quietly into the hillside, head-deep, with the hedges meeting in a green vault overhead.

The field has no path
to set the foot in.

Most days the work is a field — wide and open, no path through it, every morning a fresh decision about where to set the foot. You cross the same field four hundred times and you never quite walk the same line twice. You arrive at the gate from a slightly different angle each evening. Nothing about the field remembers you.

A holloway is the other thing. It is a line you have walked enough times that the ground itself remembers. Once it is worn in, you do not choose anymore. You step into the lane and it carries you where it has always carried you, between banks that the centuries have made. The work is in setting the foot in the same place — patiently, day after day — until the ground gives way and meets you halfway.

How it works

  1. 01

    Pick the line

    The line is the line you mean to walk every day for a long time. Not all your lines. One. From the porch step to the cup of tea on the desk. From the practice-room door to the cello. The line you are going to set the foot in patiently.

  2. 02

    Walk it once

    Today, walk the line. Not perfectly. Not for long. Set the foot in it, take it from end to end, and stop. The grass barely dims. There is no lane yet. There is only the first pressing of the soil. The first pass.

  3. 03

    Walk it again tomorrow

    And again the day after. The grass thins. The soil compacts. A trace shows from a way off. After a year of small daily passes, the line is ankle-deep. After ten, it is knee-deep. After a hundred, the hedges have arched in over it.

  4. 04

    Step into the lane

    One morning the lane has banks. You step into it and you no longer think about which way to bear; the lane carries you where it carries you. You walk along the line you wore in. You arrive at the gate from exactly the angle you have always arrived from. The work is held.

The four cuts

  • Trace. A faint dimming of the grass along a line a foot wide — the daily entry, the half-page note, the walk to the gate at first light. Always there. Never sunk an inch.
  • Hollow. An ankle-deep groove with a rounded floor — the weekly errand, the Saturday lesson, the chapter under second revision. Has begun to gather its own banks.
  • Sunken. A lane knee- to shoulder-deep, banked with fern and root — the long course taken evening by evening, the book in second draft, the practice carried twenty years.
  • Cathedral. Head-deep and over, the hedgerows meeting in a vault above — a parish road, a generational work, walked without remembering who began.

Who walks a holloway

The translator at the morning desk

Two pages a morning of the slow Italian, four years in. The field is teaching, the supervisor, the long winter. The lane is the desk between window and door, walked every morning at seven by the same slippers, ankle-deep now in the floorboards.

The cellist at the warm-up

A single open-string scale, forty seconds, every morning since 2019. Trace cut at first, ankle-deep by year three, knee-deep now. Has never been let go. The lane carries the rest of the practice in behind it.

The gardener at the long bed

The path between the cold frame and the kitchen door, walked four times a day for thirty years. Cathedral cut, hedges meeting in a vault of espaliered pear above. The wheelbarrow leaves its own narrower groove in the centre.

The runner training for the long race

The five-mile loop out the back gate, around the down, and home by the cricket field. Run four mornings a week for eight years. Sunken cut. The verges remember the bottom of the trainer.
