# Coombe

**A small page about keeping only the few things that fit in the fold,
tending them well, and letting the weather of everything else break on
the ridge and roll clean over the top.**

A coombe is the short, steep-sided valley folded into a hillside — a
hollow with a spring on its floor and its own pocket of still air, while
the wind that scours the open down above breaks on the ridge and rolls
clean over. Nothing grows on the exposed crest but gorse, bent flat. Down
in the fold, out of the wind, an oak stands a hundred years. The coombe is
not bigger than the hill; it is smaller, and that is the whole of it. It
holds only a little, and shelters it completely.

## The pitch in one breath

You treat your attention like the open down — wide, flat, room for
everything — and you let the whole weather of the week onto it at once.
Forty things stand up there in the full wind together, and not one of them
is sheltered. A coombe is the opposite shape, on purpose: a fold, not a
field. You bring a few things down out of the wind, you stand by the
spring and tend them, and you let the rest break on the ridge and roll
over. It feels like doing less. It is doing less of more things, and far
more of the few in the fold. Keep what fits. Tend it by the water. Let the
rest go over.

## The hard floor

The coombe holds six, and no more. When it is full, the next thing does
not come down — it goes over the ridge for good. The cap is the point, not
a flaw: a shelter that holds everything shelters nothing.

## How deep in the fold

- **Rim** — just over the edge, brought to the lip but still in the wind
  off the top. New, and not yet down.
- **Slope** — down off the crest and onto the slope, finding its place out
  of the worst of it. Taking hold.
- **Floor** — down on the valley floor, by the water, properly sheltered
  and in reach to tend. Established.
- **Spring** — the thing the whole coombe gathers around; the still point
  the rest of the floor shelters. The one that matters most.

## What the weather is doing

- **Exposed** — up in the wind, catching the full weather. Wants bringing
  down before it bends.
- **Sheltered** — out of the wind but not yet tended. Held by the land,
  not yet by you.
- **Tended** — watered and kept this week. The deliberate care given, the
  real work of the fold.
- **Still** — so settled it keeps its own weather. Needs nothing now; it
  simply holds.

## What's in this folder

- `index.html` — the landing page, with a hillside that folds inward into a
  steep coombe: the wind scouring the open top and rolling over the ridge,
  gorse on the exposed rim where nothing else will grow, the spring on the
  floor, and an oak standing in the still air the fold keeps for it.
- `styles.css` — a downland palette: chalk-haze on the open top, bracken
  and holloway-shade green in the fold, gorse gold, late-heather purple on
  the windy rim, and the blue-grey of the spring. Fraunces for the
  headings, Inter for the body, JetBrains Mono for the weather.
- `script.js` — one fictional coombe ("Nettlecombe") partway through a
  year, with a handful of things at different depths and weathers. A live
  *shelter line* reads the whole fold at once: it lies flat and still when
  the coombe is few and tended, and lifts and ripples as the wind gets in —
  too much brought down, or too much left exposed. Keyboard-first, and it
  remembers your fold between visits.

## Running it

Open `index.html` in a browser. No build step.
