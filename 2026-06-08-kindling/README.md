# Kindling

**A small page for the starters you keep against the cold mornings.**

A kindling pile is what the morning of a working life needs more than any
other tool. Not the big log. Not the bellows. Not the matches even,
sometimes — those are easy to find. What is hard at six in the dark, in
the cold, when nothing wants to catch, is the small dry thing that will.
The split of pine the size of a finger. The handful of birch bark. The
single dry leaf. The pile is the practice of having one already cut and
already dry.

## The pitch in one breath

Most productivity asks what the big thing is. The kindling pile asks
what the smallest thing is — the one-sentence version, the five-minute
version, the open-the-file version — that you have set aside on dry
days for the wet ones. Cold mornings come. The question on a cold
morning is not whether you have the discipline to chop a cord; it is
whether you have one dry stick within arm's reach. The pile is patience
put into small dry sticks.

## The four piles

- **Tinder** — the smallest possible start: a single sentence already
  in the notebook, a single push-up, the laptop opened at the work the
  night before. Catches at a breath. The pile under everything else.
- **Twigs** — the small first acts that catch from the tinder: a
  five-minute draft, a rough outline, the walk to the desk. Will not
  warm the room alone; will carry the spark to the splits.
- **Splits** — the half-built work waiting to be picked up: drafts
  under their last line, sketches with one corner finished, the file
  open at the place you left it on a good day. Catches from the twigs
  and starts to give heat.
- **Logs** — the substantial work that catches once the small fires
  are going: the long projects, the deep hours. Does not catch from a
  match. Needs the rest of the pile already in flame.

## The four states of a piece

- **Gathered** — just picked up and put down. Still has the wet of the
  day it was cut.
- **Drying** — a week, a month on the shelf. Losing the wet of haste.
- **Ready** — bone-dry. Will take a spark at a breath.
- **Spent** — caught, given to the work, gone. The pile is partly a
  record of which sticks already did their job.

## What's in this folder

- `index.html` — the landing page, with the kindling pile in the
  browser.
- `styles.css` — a hearth palette: birchbark, ash, char, ember, moss,
  copper-spark, bone-dry, shadow; Fraunces for headings, Inter for
  body, JetBrains Mono for the marks on the sticks.
- `script.js` — twelve seeded sticks kept by no one in particular
  through a slow turn of 2026. The piles: a one-sentence draft laid
  every Sunday night for eleven years, a five-minute warm-up at the
  bench, an opening line that lit the first novel and was put down
  again the morning after.

## Running it

Open `index.html` in a browser. No build step.
