deadbeat · adjective · /ˈdɛdbiːt/ — of an instrument or escapement, so damped that it comes to rest without a single swing past its mark; it beats once, dead, and stops

Deadbeat

A needle that swings to its reading, and stops there — no hunting, no crawl.

Every needle instrument is a tiny pendulum: a coil on a hairspring, with inertia to swing and a spring to pull it back toward zero. Send a current through it and the needle wants to swing to a new reading — but a pendulum overshoots, and a raw movement would swing past the mark, back past it, and hunt about the true value for seconds, unreadable, before it tires. So the maker adds a dashpot: an air vane, a drop of oil, or, cleverest of all, the coil's own eddy currents dragging against the field — a damping that resists in proportion to how fast the needle moves. Too little and it hunts; too much and it crawls up to the mark so slowly the reading comes late. Exactly enough and it is deadbeat: it beats once, lands on the reading, and dies.

Where the reading comes from

A spring to swing, a dashpot to stop

The hairspring is the will: it sets a definite rest angle for each current, and pulls the needle toward it. But a spring and a mass together are an oscillator — pull the needle off and let go, and it will not simply arrive, it will swing through and past, because at the mark it is moving fastest and cannot stop on a coin. That overshoot is the enemy of a fast, honest reading: a needle still swinging is a needle you cannot trust.

A spring tells the needle where to go. A dashpot tells it when to stop. Without the second, the first is only a promise the needle keeps overshooting.

The dashpot fights the needle's speed, F = c·v, so it is strongest exactly at the mark, where the needle moves fastest, and gentlest at the ends of the swing, where it barely moves. That is precisely the manners a reading wants: let the needle come quickly, then take the overshoot out of it at the last instant. The whole art of a good movement is choosing how much drag — enough to kill the swing, not so much that the needle forgets it was ever in a hurry.

The one relation, read three ways

Natural swing, damping, arrival

Take the movement's inertia m, the hairspring k, and the damping c. Idealise the pivot as frictionless, and the swing to a new reading is a second-order step response, read three ways off the damping ratio:

  • It has a natural swing. ωn = √(k ⁄ m) — how fast the needle and hairspring would oscillate, undamped. A lighter movement or a stiffer spring swings faster and settles sooner, but overshoots harder if undamped.
  • Damping is the one dial. ζ = c ⁄ (2·√(k·m)) — the drag measured against that swing. Below one the needle hunts: it overshoots the mark and swings back, again and again. Above one it crawls: it creeps to the mark with no overshoot but takes its time. At exactly one — deadbeat — it reaches the mark in the least time with no swing past it at all.
  • The dashpot stores nothing. The swing's energy goes to heat — in the vane's air, the oil, or the eddy currents in the coil, P = c·v² — and never comes back. That is why damping can only ever end a swing, never start one, and why a deadbeat movement is a movement that forgets its momentum exactly at the reading.

Those three are one fact. The swing is set by the movement and the spring; the stopping by the drag against that swing; and the drag only spends. Hunt and the reading is unreadable; crawl and it is late; and the fastest honest reading is the one that beats once — the least damping that lands the needle without a swing.

A needle that hunts cannot be read. A needle that crawls reads too late. Deadbeat is the single setting that is neither: once to the mark, and done.

Four arrivals, from a hunt to a needle that never gets there

Around the one mark

  1. Hunts. Almost no damping. The needle swings past the mark and back, over and over, oscillating about the true value — you must wait and average by eye, or wait for it to tire. The reading is there, buried in a swing you cannot stop.
  2. Swings. Some damping, not enough. ζ < 1: the needle overshoots once or twice and settles after a wobble. Readable, but not at a glance, and not the instant it moves.
  3. Reads. The damping sized to the movement, ζ ≈ 1 — deadbeat. The needle sweeps to the mark and stops there, no overshoot, in the least time any setting allows. The whole aim of a good instrument: the true value, now, once.
  4. Crawls. Far too much damping. ζ ≫ 1: the drag smothers the swing so the needle creeps toward the mark and never quite arrives before you have given up on it. A reading too late to use is as good as no reading at all.

Who sets it, and for what

The same damping, three ways

The instrument maker

wants it deadbeat

Tunes the vane or the eddy-current damping so the needle lands on the mark once and stops — ζ ≈ 1 — the fastest reading with no swing to average out. A galvanometer's whole reputation is how cleanly its needle settles.

The clockmaker

wants no recoil

Knows the same word from the deadbeat escapement: a pallet face cut so the escape wheel is held dead between beats, not shoved backward. Damping and geometry meeting the same demand — take the overshoot out, keep the count honest.

The panel reader

wants a glance

Reads a moving needle a hundred times a shift and trusts it only if it settles at once. A hunting gauge hides its value in a blur; a crawling one lags the thing it measures. Deadbeat is what lets a glance be a reading.

The discipline in the word

To beat once, and be still

Deadbeat is the finest word of the three, because it names not a part but a virtue: to arrive and be still, in one motion, with nothing left over. A needle earns it, a clock's escapement earns it, and both are asking the same thing of a dashpot — meet the motion in proportion to its speed, take exactly the overshoot out and no more, and spend what you take on heat that never returns. Too little and it hunts; too much and it crawls; and the best is not the most damping but the least that beats once. It is the same discipline as a door that lands at its latch and a car that settles after a bump: the swing must be met and ended, cleanly, at the mark — and the mark, for a needle, is the truth it was built to tell.
