# Brine

**A small page for keeping what you don't act on green.**

Not every idea should be eaten the day it is cut. You put the raw
thing down in salt, weight it under a clean stone on a plate, and
carry it to the cool dark of the cellar, and forget it. There it
draws its own water, the brine comes up cloudy over the top, and you
leave it well alone. Now and then you go down to turn it and skim the
bloom. One winter — two winters — the green has soured slow into
something cured that keeps for years, and is better than anything you
could have made of it fresh. Brine is a note cellar for the raw,
unripe ideas that are better preserved and turned slowly than acted on
the hour you have them.

## The pitch in one breath

Most ideas eaten green go off by Friday — acted on raw, the heat goes
out of them and the obvious flaw surfaces, and what looked like a feast
on Monday you'd rather not have served by the weekend. Brine is the
other thing. You put the raw notion down in salt, under a weight, in
the cool dark where you cannot fuss at it; you go down only to turn it
and skim the bloom; and the good sour comes slowly, on its own time.
You cannot hurry a cure. You put it down, you turn it, and one winter
it is ready to take down.

## The four levels

- **Raw** — just cut, green, still on the board, only just put aside.
  The notion scribbled this week, the empty crock with one line on the
  lid. Not yet under the salt; act on it now and it spoils.
- **Crock** — down in the salt under a weight, drawing its own water,
  the brine just clouding up. The idea you've committed to keeping
  rather than doing. Real, under at last, but nowhere near ready.
- **Cellar** — kept in the cool dark and turned over a season, the good
  sour coming on. A draft, a plan, a project working its slow change
  while you leave it be and go down only to skim.
- **Larder** — cured, shelved, keeps for years, ready to take down when
  wanted. A sourdough mother fed a decade, a vintage laid down at a
  birth. The deep idea that only got better for the waiting.

## The four conditions

- **Fresh** — just put down, the brine barely up, the salt only
  starting to draw. It has only this moment gone under, and there is
  nothing to do but leave it.
- **Salting** — drawing the water out, the brine clouding, the crock
  settling. Nothing to watch, nothing to hurry — it is taking the salt
  it needs to keep.
- **Souring** — the slow working change, the good sour coming on, turned
  over a season. The flavour that was impossible in the green is coming
  now. The change is being made, underneath, on its own time.
- **Cured** — soured through and set, keeping, ready to take down and
  use. It will keep for years. Some crocks come good in a season. A few
  want longer in the dark.

## What's in this folder

- `index.html` — the landing page, with a keeper's cellar scene: a row
  of stoneware crocks on a cool dark stone shelf, one open with a
  weighting-stone on a plate holding produce under cloudy brine, a
  long wooden skimmer leaning against it, a hanging bunch of dill and
  bay, dim lamplight from above.
- `styles.css` — a brine palette: cellar cream, stoneware crock brown,
  ferment amber, soured russet, the dark larder shelf, pale dill, bay
  green, brine grey-green, weight-stone grey, salt white. Fraunces for
  the headings, Inter for the body, JetBrains Mono for the turns.
- `script.js` — twelve crocks across a fictional keeper's note cellar
  in the middle of June 2026. The sourdough mother fed since 2016, the
  raw business idea salted only this week, the cask laid down the cold
  spring a daughter was born, the bad idea that went off in the warm
  and was thrown out at Whitsun.
