# Marram

**A small page for binding what drifts, one tuft at a time.**

Marram is the blue-green grass that lives where nothing else will — on
bare, blowing sand, in salt wind, with the ground moving under it. You
cannot hold a dune by standing on it. You push in one tuft and it looks
like nothing, a few blades bent flat by the gale; but under the surface
it is running, sending pale rhizomes a yard out and a yard down,
throwing new shoots up through the very sand that tries to bury it.
Tuft finds tuft, the runners cross, and a season on the loose face has
knitted into a mat that holds its shape in a winter storm — and
shelters everything you plant in the slack behind it.

## The pitch in one breath

A new intention is loose sand. You scatter it on a Monday full of
resolve, and the first ordinary wind — a bad night, a busy week — takes
it by Thursday. You did not lack the will; you lacked the roots. Marram
is the other way. You do not hold the whole hill at once. You plant one
tuft, low and sheltered, and leave it to send its runners down where no
one can watch. Season by season the mat closes, stops needing you, and
holds against the gale on its own. You cannot hurry a dune into
holding. You can only keep planting tufts.

## The four levels

- **Seed** — a single intention just pushed into the sand, nothing
  showing yet. The first dark morning, the resolution said out loud
  once. Loose; the next ordinary wind could take it, and often does.
- **Tuft** — a small clump that has taken, a few blades up and a runner
  gone down. The practice you have actually done a handful of times.
  Real now, holding a little sand, but still on its own.
- **Sward** — a continuous mat of grass binding a whole stretch of
  slope. A habit knitted to its neighbours, holding its shape through
  weather. The thing you no longer have to decide to do.
- **Dune** — a whole stabilised dune system that now shelters what is
  planted behind it. A way of living, years deep, that holds against
  the gale and makes the next hard thing easy.

## The four conditions

- **Loose** — the sand still moving, nothing holding yet. Just planted,
  exposed, gripping nothing. One bad week and there is no sign you were
  ever there.
- **Rooting** — sending runners down, taking hold. The slow work no one
  sees, underground, a grain of sand at a time. Nothing to perform and
  nothing to post — just come back and let it root.
- **Knitting** — binding its neighbours, the mat closing. The tufts
  have run together; the morning page leans on the early night that
  leans on the walk. A practice with neighbours, not a practice alone.
- **Bound** — holds against the gale. Years deep, throwing its own
  shoots, taking its own drift, breaking the wind for everything in the
  slack behind it. You held the sand once; now the sand holds you.

## What's in this folder

- `index.html` — the landing page, with a stretch of dune coast: the
  long blown sweep of the foredune, a line of chestnut sand-fence
  catching drift, a scoured-out blowout, the knitted marram on the high
  face and a single new tuft low on the bare slope, the sea-haze behind.
- `styles.css` — a marram palette: pale dry sand, marram blue-green,
  silvered blade, wet-sand brown, shell grey, sea-haze, dark dune-slack,
  foam cream. Fraunces for the headings, Inter for the body, JetBrains
  Mono for the gales.
- `script.js` — twelve plantings across a fictional stretch of links
  and foredune on the Gwendra Warren coast in the middle of June 2026.
  The warden's grandfather's dune planted since 1958, the single seed
  pushed into the bare slip-face this March, the blowout where a good
  sward was lost in one March storm.

## Running it

Open `index.html` in a browser. No build step.
