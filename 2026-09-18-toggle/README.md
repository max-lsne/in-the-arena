# Toggle

**Pin two stiff links end to end at a middle knee, fix one far end and let the
other bear on the work, and push the knee sideways toward the straight line the
links want to make. As they straighten they drive their far ends apart, and
because the push turns a wide angle into a shrinking one, a light shove on the
knee becomes a heavy **thrust** at the ends — growing without limit as the links
come straight. At the **dead centre**, in line, the thrust is unbounded and the
travel has shrunk to nothing; carried a hair past it against a stop, the load's
own push holds the joint shut with no catch. The knee angle is the one dial, and
it sets the thrust, the travel, and whether the joint drives or merely holds.**

A toggle is a lever whose ratio is not fixed but sweeps as it works. The word is
old and nautical — a *toggle* was a crosspin through an eye — and it names the
mechanism by its knee: two links and the pin between them. What surprises is that
one falling angle does everything, and that the joint's whole gift, and its whole
danger, is the same fact — its advantage has no ceiling.

## The one relation

Let each link make an angle `θ` with the straight line through the two far pins,
and let the push on the knee be `P`. Balance the knee, ideal and frictionless, and
the whole of it is three short lines:

- **The thrust.** The push is shared into compression along the two links and
  reappears at the ends as `Q = P ⁄ (2·tan θ)`, so the mechanical advantage is
  `1 ⁄ (2·tan θ)` — one at `θ ≈ 26.6°`, and climbing without limit as `θ → 0`.
  Wider than that and the toggle is a mechanical *disadvantage*, delivering less
  than it is given.
- **The link.** Each link carries a compression `S = P ⁄ (2·sin θ)` — the force
  the knee pin passes and the link must not buckle under. It runs away a touch
  faster than the thrust, so the parts feel the closing before the work does.
- **The travel.** Force is never free. The ends advance only `2·tan θ` for each
  step of the knee, so at the dead centre the thrust is unbounded and the delivered
  travel is nil. A toggle cannot both push hard and move far; the angle chooses.

So the knee angle sweeps between two useless ends. Open it wide and the toggle
idles — travel, no advantage. Push it onto the dead centre and it *takes charge* —
the thrust and the link force spike toward the parts' limits, a hair of extra push
does great harm, and the joint, carried just over, holds itself with no hand on it.
Between idle and overload is the angle a fitter sets: flat enough to bite, open
enough to keep the pins and links inside their limits and the stroke under control.

## The bench

A toggle joint driven shut: two **links** pinned at a knee, a warm **push** on the
knee square to their line, and the cold **thrust** it delivers at the ends into the
work, the links coloured heavier and darker as they near their limit. Pick the
**mechanism** — a hand hold-down clamp, a coining press, a stone crusher with its
sacrificial plate, an over-centre latch — set the **push** as a share of the
mechanism's design input, and sweep the **angle** from a wide-open `60°` down
through the working bite to the `2°` edge of dead centre. The gauge reads the force
in each part against its limit, and the push the mechanism safely takes at this
angle. Press *Find the angle* to set the knee to the flattest angle that still
bites within every part's limit; *Reset* returns it to the wide, force-losing press
it opens on.

The four mechanisms give first in a different part: the **clamp** buckles its
slender links, the **press** shears its knee pin, the **crusher** folds its
sacrificial toggle-plate, the **latch** tears its light frame — and none of it is
an angle's to fix, because the toggle sets how the push is multiplied, not how
large it is.
