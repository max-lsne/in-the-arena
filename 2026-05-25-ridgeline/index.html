Lists are flat.
Ridges have a shape.

A backlog is a heap. Forty items in a column, all asking the same way. At the end of the week the ones you finished look like the ones you didn't. The list does not know which one was the summit. It does not even know there was one.

A ridge is a shape. The summit is the one decision the project tips over — once crossed, the work drops down one valley and not the other. The saddles are the smaller calls that give the ridge its line. The approaches are the climbs that bring you near enough to see the summit for what it is. The week reads true when the ridge is named first.

How it works

    Name the summit

    One decision this week. The one that, once made, sends the project down one valley and not the other. Name it first.

  2. 02

    Sketch the saddles

    The smaller passes. A few — five, maybe seven. Each one a call you can take either way, and each one giving the ridge its line.

  3. 03

    Walk the approaches

    The climbs. The work that brings you near enough to a summit to see it for what it really is. Sight before you cross.

  4. 04

    Cross, skirt, or return

    Some crossings you make. Some you go around — honest if you meant it. Some you walk back over later. The ridge knows.

The three points on a ridge

  • Summit. The one decision the week is built around. Only one. Crossed last. From here the project tips one way and not the other.
  • Saddle. A low pass between summits. A smaller call. Approachable from either side; the ridge gets its shape from these.
  • Approach. The climb that gets you near enough to a summit to see it for what it is. Sight before you cross.

Who walks a ridge

The product lead before a launch

The summit is shipping to ten people the week before the rest. The saddles are the price page, the changelog format, the screenshot for the card. Once the summit is crossed the saddles read different. They were always the saddles.

The clinician choosing a protocol

The summit is the one switch in the morning routine. The approaches are the three weeks of reading and the notebook in the white coat. The saddles are the small things the team will change anyway; the summit is the call only this clinician can make.

The graduate student in the last fall

The summit is the defense date. The saddles are the chapter order and which paper goes in the appendix. The approaches are the long mornings of writing and the longer afternoons of rereading. The ridge runs straight through October.

The small-shop founder before a price change

The summit is the email to forty regulars. The saddles are the wording, the date, the freebie. The approaches are the spreadsheet and the late kitchen conversations. After the summit the shop reads different to itself.
