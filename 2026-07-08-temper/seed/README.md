# Seed

**Run the whole vat hot and pour it in a hurry and it blooms — streaky, grey,
soft, dull. Seed a few, hold them in the window, and they set with a mirror
gloss and a clean snap.**

Chocolate arrives already made; tempering is not about flavour, it is about
*crystal*. Cocoa butter can set in several crystal forms, and only one — the
tight, well-ordered Form V — gives a mirror shine, a clean snap, and a set that
keeps at room temperature without turning to dust. To get it, the chocolatier
melts the chocolate right down so every old crystal is gone, cools it working on
a marble slab, and *seeds* it with a little already-tempered chocolate so the
right crystal has something to grow on. Then comes the part no one has patience
for: *holding* it. Tempered chocolate lives in a window barely two degrees wide,
and it has to be kept there — stirred, tested, warmed and cooled by breaths —
until it sets. Get greedy, run the whole vat hot and pour it before it is ready,
and the wrong crystals take instead: it *blooms*, setting streaky and grey and
soft, and it never snaps. The gloss was never in tempering the whole vat. It was
in seeding a little and holding it in the window.

Seed is a small page that holds that shape. It is a board for the few things
that only come glossy and sound when they are cooled slow, seeded, and held —
the ones you are keeping in the window — against the many you ran hot and poured
in a hurry and let bloom.

## The two axes

A bowl is read two ways at once, and they are not the same reading.

**Stage** — how far along the chocolate has come:

- **Raw** — solid, set some old careless way; not yet melted.
- **Melted** — run right down, warm and slack; every old crystal gone.
- **Seeded** — cooled working and seeded; the tight crystal taking hold.
- **Set** — held in the window and set; a mirror gloss and a clean snap.

**Condition** — how it is behaving in the window, right now:

- **Dull** — untempered or bloomed; matte, grey, no shine at all.
- **Loose** — warm, drifted above the window; the seed melting back out.
- **Satin** — held in the window, coming to a sheen; the crystal setting.
- **Snap** — set glossy and sound; breaks clean with a bright edge.

The two come apart all the time. A bowl can be well *seeded* and still only
*loose* — the crystal taking, but the bowl run warm and drifting out of the
window. It can be freshly *melted* and left *dull* — good chocolate waiting, not
yet seeded and not yet spoiled. How far along it has come and how it is behaving
this minute are different questions, and the chocolatier asks both every time he
draws a smear across a knife.

## The bench holds six

Tempered chocolate lives in a window barely two degrees wide, and holding a bowl
there is constant, fiddly work — a touch too warm and the seed melts out, a
touch too cool and it thickens and sets before you can use it. A chocolatier can
only hold so many bowls in that window at once. Run the whole vat hot and pour
it all and the far bowls drift out while you mind the near one — the more you
temper at once, the more you let bloom. So the bench has a hard ceiling, and the
ceiling is the point: **Seed holds six bowls.** When the bench is full the next
batch waits raw and solid, out of the pan, until one bowl has set to a snap and
been used.
