What comes of seeding the set slow

Run the whole vat hot,
and it blooms grey and dull.

Chocolate is already made when it reaches you — the temper is not about flavour, it is about crystal. Melt it and the cocoa butter can set in any of several forms, and only one, the tight glossy Form V, gives a mirror shine and a clean snap and keeps. To get it you melt the chocolate right down so the old crystal is gone, cool it working on a slab, and seed it with a little tempered chocolate so the right crystal takes — then hold it in a window barely two degrees wide until it sets. Get greedy, run the whole vat hot and pour it in a hurry, and the wrong crystals grow: it blooms — streaky, dull, grey, soft, and it never snaps. So you temper a few bowls, seeded and held, and keep each in the window.

How it works

Stop running the whole vat hot. Seed a few, and hold them in the window.

01

Melt it right down, and lose the old crystal

A thing you mean to bring right by seeding starts raw — solid, set some old careless way — and then melted right down, warm and slack, every old crystal gone. Loose and formless, it is ready to be seeded, not yet holding any shape.

02

Cool it working, and seed the set

Seeding is minding a set. You move it along — raw, melted, seeded, set — and you read how it is tempering right now: dull, loose, satin, snap. How far along it has come and how it is behaving in the window this minute are not the same question.

03

Hold the window, and let it come to a snap

A gloss is held, not hurried. Seed more bowls than you can hold and the far ones drift out of the window unwatched, and bloom grey and dull. A few, seeded and kept in their two degrees, come off with a mirror shine and a clean snap.

The bench

A chocolatier tempers only as many bowls as he can hold in the window.

Tempered chocolate lives in a window barely two degrees wide — a touch too warm and the seed melts back out and it goes loose; a touch too cool and it thickens and sets before you can use it. Holding a bowl there is constant, fiddly work: stirring, testing a smear on a knife, warming and cooling by breaths. A chocolatier can only hold so many bowls in that window at once. Run the whole vat hot and pour it all and the far bowls drift out while you mind the near one — the more you temper at once, the more you let bloom. The cure is never more bowls. It is fewer, kept close, and each held in its window.

So the bench has a hard ceiling, and the ceiling is the point. Seed holds six bowls. When the bench is full the next batch waits raw and solid, out of the pan, until one bowl has set to a snap and been used. A bench tempered beyond what one pair of hands can hold would bloom, bowl by bowl, before a single one was ever glossy.

The whole-vat pourer

A dozen bowls all run hot and poured at once, drifting out of the window unwatched while you stir the wrong one. Seed asks which six you are actually holding, and lets the rest wait raw and solid.

The slow setter

A craft, a piece of writing, a friendship — things that only come glossy and sound when they are cooled slow, seeded, and held in the window. Seed is built for the minutes of holding.

The one whose batch bloomed
