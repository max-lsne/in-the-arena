# Temper

**Three small pages, made the same morning, for the same idea seen from three
trades: a made thing is finished by a low, watched second working — not by more
force, and not by putting the whole shop on the heat at once.**

Most days in this folder are one page. Some mornings the idea arrives already
banked in threes. *Temper* is the word three trades reach for when the making
is not the making but the *finishing*: the piece is already formed — hardened,
melted, thrown together — and now it has to be brought to its working state by a
slow, attended, deliberate conditioning that you cannot hurry and cannot force.
Rush the temper and you undo the whole piece; put more on the heat than you can
stand over and the far ones go while your back is turned.

Three trades temper a thing they each mean differently, and each learned the
same lesson the hard way. The smith draws the temper of a quenched blade — it
comes off the quench glass-hard and brittle, and he sets it back over a low heat
and *watches the colour run*, straw to bronze to blue, pulling it at the exact
shade that trades a sliver of hardness for the toughness that keeps an edge from
snapping. The confectioner tempers chocolate — melts it right down, then cools
it working and *seeds* it so the tight, glossy crystal takes, and holds it in a
narrow window until it sets with a mirror gloss and a clean snap. The potter
tempers clay — *wedges* it, cutting and stacking a modest ball in a whole-body
rhythm to drive the trapped air out and even the body, because a pot thrown from
unwedged clay flies off the wheel or bursts in the kiln.

They start in different places — a forge, a slab, a bench — and they meet here,
because they are all the one habit said in three trades: **the piece is finished
low and slow and watched, on a few you can stand over, or it is not finished at
all.**

## The three

- **[Draw](./draw/)** — *set it back over a low heat, and pull it at the colour.*
  A quenched blade is hard and brittle; throw it back in the fire hot and the
  temper colours race past blue and you have drawn it out dead soft, and a whole
  forge of irons goes at once while you watch the wrong one. Draw a few, over a
  low heat, and read the running colour on each — pale straw, gold, bronze,
  blue — pulling each at its shade. A small page for the few things you are
  bringing to a working toughness by a slow second heat you stand over, against
  the whole forge thrown on the fire at once and drawn to nothing.
  Hard → Bright → Drawing → Let. Cold → Running → Blued → Tough.

- **[Seed](./seed/)** — *cool it working, seed the set, and hold the window.*
  Run the whole vat hot and pour it in a hurry and it blooms — sets streaky,
  grey, soft, dull, and never snaps. Cool a few bowls working, seed each with a
  little tempered chocolate so the tight crystal takes, and hold them in the
  narrow window and they set with a mirror gloss and a clean break. A small page
  for the few things that only come glossy and sound when they are cooled slow,
  seeded, and held — against the whole vat run hot and poured before it is
  ready.
  Raw → Melted → Seeded → Set. Dull → Loose → Satin → Snap.

- **[Wedge](./wedge/)** — *cut it, stack it, and drive the air out first.*
  Slam a whole barrow of wet clay together and throw it in a hurry and the
  hidden air fights the wheel, throws the pot off true, and bursts it in the
  kiln — taking its neighbours with it. Wedge a modest ball in a patient
  rhythm — cut, stack, press — until the body is even and the air is gone, and
  it centres sweet and comes up true. A small page for the few things you make
  sound by the plain unglamorous work done before the wheel, against the heap
  thrown together wet and hoped over.
  Wet → Wedging → Evened → Thrown. Short → Airy → Slack → Plastic.

## The shared shape

Each page is the same small machine the folder keeps building: a hero, the
problem stated plainly, three steps, an interactive board of a dozen seeded
entries you can sort, advance, and reset, four stages and four conditions, a
handful of people who keep one of these for real, and a quiet waitlist that
promises one email and no streaks. Two of the three carry a live gauge that
reads the whole board at once and only stays calm if you keep it to a tended
few — the smith's colour line and the confectioner's gloss line. The potter's
page stays quiet, because clay tells you it was rushed only later, in the fire.
No build step — open any `index.html` in a browser. It all lives in your
browser; nothing leaves it.
