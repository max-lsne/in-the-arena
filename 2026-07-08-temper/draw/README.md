# Draw

**Throw it back hot and the colour races past blue and the temper draws out
dead soft. Draw a few over a low heat, pull each at its shade, and they come
tough.**

A blade comes off the quench *glass-hard* — hard enough to take a keen edge,
and brittle enough to snap clean across if you look at it wrong. It is not
finished; it is dangerous. To bring it right the smith *draws* the temper. He
grinds the black scale off until the steel is bright, sets the blade back over a
low, even heat, and watches the colour crawl up the polished metal as it warms:
a pale straw first, then a full gold, a bronze, a peacock purple, and finally a
spring blue. Each colour is a temper — a little softer, a little tougher than
the last — and the whole craft is pulling the iron from the heat at the *exact*
shade the job wants, trading a sliver of hardness for the toughness that lets an
edge flex instead of chipping. Get greedy, throw it back on a hot fire to hurry
it, and the colours race up and past blue in a single breath: the temper draws
clean out, the steel goes dead soft, and a day's forging is scrap. So you draw a
*few*, over a low heat, and stand over each one until its colour is right.

Draw is a small page that holds that shape. It is a board for the few things
that only come tough when they are drawn slow and pulled at the right shade —
the ones you are standing over — against the many you threw back on hot in a
hurry and ran past blue.

## The two axes

An iron is read two ways at once, and they are not the same reading.

**Stage** — how far along the steel has come:

- **Hard** — off the quench, glass-hard and brittle; not yet drawn.
- **Bright** — ground and polished clean, so a temper colour can be read.
- **Drawing** — set back over a low heat, the colour crawling up the steel.
- **Let** — pulled at its colour and let down; tempered, tough, finished.

**Condition** — what colour it is running, right now:

- **Cold** — bright and cold, no colour yet; still hard, still brittle.
- **Running** — the colour racing up the steel; hot, and easy to overshoot.
- **Blued** — caught at a spring blue; the temper taking, tough and springy.
- **Tough** — drawn even and settled all through; bends and springs back.

The two come apart all the time. An iron can be well into the *drawing* and
still only *running* — the colour crawling up, not yet caught at its shade. It
can be freshly *hard* and left *cold* — good hard steel waiting, not yet thrown
back on and run soft. How far along it has come and what colour it is running
this minute are different questions, and the smith asks both every time his eye
falls on the fire.

## The forge holds six

A temper colour runs in seconds and does not come back once it has passed its
shade — overshoot it and you must harden the piece and start the whole draw
again. So drawing is standing over the heat with your eye on the steel, pulling
each iron at the moment its colour is right. That is close, unhurried work, and
a smith can only watch so many irons at once. Throw the whole forge on the fire
and the far ones run past blue while you are pulling the near one — the more you
set on, the more you draw out soft. So the forge has a hard ceiling, and the
ceiling is the point: **Draw holds six irons.** When the forge is full the next
blade waits hard and bright on the bench, out of the fire, until one iron has
been drawn and pulled and set aside.
