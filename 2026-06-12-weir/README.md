# Weir

**A small page for the long stone that lifts the water two feet.**

A weir is the lowest possible dam — a course of dressed stone laid flat
across a stream so that the water, gently held, rises behind it and
spills cleanly over the crest. It does not stop the river. It only
lifts the water a couple of feet, so that some of it can be drawn off
to the level — patient, steady, exactly enough to do the slow work
without the rest of the river noticing.

## The pitch in one breath

Most days the work is a river — too fast, too low, never the same
level twice. The weir does not stop the river. It lifts the water two
feet behind a long shallow stone, and the rest of the river goes
downstream as it always did. What is held is held to a level, and
what spills, spills clean and even and steady all summer.

## The four cuts

- **Notch** — a V-cut in the crest, finger-width, for the lowest
  flow: the daily entry, the half-page note, the warm-up scale.
  Always there. Always trickling. Always at level.
- **Sill** — a flat broad cut, a thumb-deep brim: the weekly
  practice, the Saturday workshop, the chapter under second
  revision. Spills steady when the water rises that high, which
  is most weeks.
- **Crest** — the full dressed length of the weir, a season patient
  and quiet: the long course taken evening by evening, the book in
  the second draft, the slow recovery from injury, the garden put
  in for the year.
- **Compound** — a stepped weir of two or three falls down the
  hillside: a decade work, a rebuilt practice, a second career.
  Built in courses, one step a year, the water held longer at each
  level before it spills to the next.

## The four states of a hold

- **Below the sill** — the water is rising behind the weir, gathering,
  not yet at the level of the crest. The hold is filling.
- **At the crest** — the water is brimming, lipping the stone, about
  to spill. The hold is full. Nothing is yet leaving.
- **Spilling** — the water is over the crest, clean and steady. The
  hold is doing its work in the open air.
- **Drawn off** — the held water has gone through the sluice gate
  on the side, off to the wheel or the field or the cup. The work
  is used. The pool will fill again.

## What's in this folder

- `index.html` — the landing page, with a small weir at the bend of
  a small river, a long stone crest brimming, a quiet line of water
  lipping the stone and spilling onto a paved apron downstream.
- `styles.css` — a weir palette: slate, river, river-deep, willow,
  reed, brick, lichen, stone-warm, shadow, ink; Fraunces for
  headings, Inter for body, JetBrains Mono for the gauge marks on
  the side of the stone.
- `script.js` — twelve seeded holds along a slow turn of the spring
  of 2026. A thesis chapter at the crest, three years in. A weekly
  scale that has been spilling, in a thin even sheet, every Sunday
  since 2019. A compound work of three steps, the lowest already in
  use, the upper two still filling quietly.
