What keeps a flock on unfenced ground

A fence holds sheep in.
A heft holds them home.

On the open hill there are no walls. A heft is the stretch of fell a flock has learned, over generations, as its own ground — and it stays there not because anything pens it, but because the knowledge of where home is gets passed down, ewe to lamb, until the hill itself does the holding. Take the flock off and the hefting is lost; it takes years to teach a bare hill again. The whole idea is quiet and slow: a few things can keep you without being fenced — if you let them settle long enough to be learned.

How it works

Stop herding. Let a few things learn the ground.

01

Bring it onto the fell

A thing you mean to keep starts as a stray — up on the bare line, wandered in, not yet holding anywhere. You walk it down onto the ground you want it to learn.

02

Let it settle, and mind it

Settling is slow. You move it down the fell — grazing, settled, hefted — and you mind its condition week to week: lean, minded, thriving, in fine fettle. The two are not the same thing.

03

Read the drift

The fell carries only so much. Overstock it, or let too many go loose and lean, and the flock starts to drift. One line reads the whole heft — steady when it holds, scattering when it doesn't.

The fell

A hill carries what it can teach, and no more.

Hefting is not free grazing. A fell has a stocking it can hold in good heart; push past it and the whole flock goes lean, the ground gets poached, and the learned belonging that took years to lay down starts to come apart. Sheep that should know exactly where they stand begin to wander. The cure is never more sheep. It is fewer, minded better, on ground they have properly learned.

So the board has a hard ceiling, and the ceiling is the point. Heft holds six. When the fell is full, the next thing does not get walked on — it stays off the hill until something else goes off for good. A heft that tried to carry everything would teach nothing its ground.

Who keeps one

For people tired of fencing the same things in twice.

The over-committed

Forty open loops, all minded by force of will. A heft asks which six are worth teaching the ground, and lets the rest off the hill.

The slow builder

A craft, a relationship, a body of work — things that only hold once they've been let to settle for years. Heft is built for the long graze.
