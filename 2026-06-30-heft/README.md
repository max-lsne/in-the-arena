# Heft

**A small page about keeping a few things on ground they have learned, so
that they hold you the way a hefted flock holds a hill — without fences,
because the knowing has settled in.**

On the open fell there are no walls. A *heft* is the stretch of unfenced
hill a flock has learned, over generations, as its own ground. The ewes
stay not because anything pens them but because the knowledge of where home
is gets passed down, mother to lamb, until the hill itself does the
holding. Take the flock off and the hefting is lost; it takes years to
teach a bare hill again. A fence holds sheep in. A heft holds them home.

## The pitch in one breath

You hold your commitments the way a hill farmer would hold sheep with no
walls left: by force of constant herding. Forty things, all minded by will,
each one a stray the moment you look away. A heft is the opposite, on
purpose. You walk a few things onto ground you mean for them to learn, you
let them settle — slowly, over a season, not a day — and you mind their
condition until the ground itself does the holding. It feels like doing
less. It is keeping fewer, on better ground, until they no longer need the
fence.

## The hard floor

The fell holds six, and no more. A hill carries only so much in good heart;
overstock it and the whole flock goes lean, the ground gets poached, and
the hefting you spent years laying down begins to come apart. When the fell
is full, the next thing stays off the hill until something else goes off
for good. The cap is the point, not a flaw: a heft that tried to carry
everything would teach nothing its ground.

## How far onto the fell

- **Stray** — up on the bare line, wandered in, holding nowhere yet. New,
  and not yet walked down.
- **Grazing** — down on the fell but loose; here, taking the ground, but
  not yet kept to it.
- **Settled** — keeping to its own lines now, finding the same ground day
  after day. Taking hold.
- **Hefted** — the ground does the holding. It knows where home is and will
  not drift. The thing you no longer have to fence.

## How it's faring

- **Lean** — underfed and thin, slipping toward the bare line. Wants minding
  before it strays.
- **Minded** — looked after this week. Held by your attention, deliberately
  given.
- **Thriving** — in good flesh and taking the ground well. Faring on its own
  strength.
- **Fettle** — in fine fettle: so settled and well it keeps its own
  condition. Needs nothing now but to be left to it.

Depth and condition are not the same axis, and the page keeps them apart on
purpose. A thing can be deeply *hefted* and gone *lean* — rooted, but
neglected. Another can be *thriving* and still a *stray* — full of life, but
holding nowhere yet.

## What's in this folder

- `index.html` — the landing page, with a fell that climbs in folded ridges:
  the flock settled low and together on the green it has learned, the bare
  line above where hefting fails and a single stray stands off on its own,
  and a small cairn for the ground to gather around.
- `styles.css` — a hill-farming palette: moor-grass fawn and chalk on the
  open ground, drystone slate, fleece cream, bracken and a deep hefted green
  down on the learned fell, late heather on the bare line. Fraunces for the
  headings, Inter for the body, JetBrains Mono for the condition tags.
- `script.js` — one fictional fell ("Cawdale Fell") partway through a
  season, with a handful of things at different depths and conditions. A
  live *drift line* reads the whole heft at once: it lies flat and steady
  when the flock is hefted and in fettle, and lifts and scatters as the
  sheep go loose — too many strays, too much gone lean, or the fell
  overstocked. Keyboard-first, and it remembers your ground between visits.

## Running it

Open `index.html` in a browser. No build step.
