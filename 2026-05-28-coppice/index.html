The cut is
the practice.

A coppice is a managed woodland where trees are cut to the stool and left to regrow. The cut is not loss. The cut is the practice. A coppiced ash can outlive an uncut one by a thousand years. Coppice is a small page for the few things in a life that grow back better for being cut.

Planners
are for forests.

Most planners want a line going up. They draw a forest: every practice taller than the last, every quarter adding rings. The line punishes the year you cut the project on purpose. The line does not believe in fallow. The line is the wrong shape.

A coppice is the right shape. The line goes down on purpose, then up, then down again, on a slow turn. The old practices in a coppiced life are not the unbroken ones — they are the ones that have been cut and grown back, cut and grown back, for years. The cut is what keeps them young. The cut is the work.

How it works

  1. 01

    Name a stool

    One thing — a practice, a project, a slow attachment — you mean to keep on a long cycle. Not a goal. A stool.

  2. 02

    Pick a stand

    Inner for the practices you cut in yourself. Working for the projects. Edge for the slow attachments at the edge of your life.

  3. 03

    Cut when the cycle says

    Set it back to a stool on purpose. A year, three years, seven. The cut is named. The regrowth is named. Both are the work.

  4. 04

    Leave a standard

    Some trees the coppicer never cuts. The standards are the canopy, the seed, the proof. Not everything is for cutting.

The three stands

  • Inner. What you cut back in yourself: the morning page, the evening walk, the practice you fell so it could come back greener.
  • Working. Projects you cut back on purpose: the side book set down for a year, the newsletter rested in winter.
  • Edge. Slow attachments at the edge of your life: the Tuesday call, the letter habit, the long Sunday with one person.

Who keeps a wood

The novelist between the third and fourth book

Cuts the manuscript every January. Lets it lie under leaves until June. The novel that took seven years was cut six times. The novel that took two years has never been cut. The seven-year one will outlast her.

The therapist with a Tuesday call of fifteen years

Two standards in the edge stand — one Tuesday call, one Sunday meal. Three shoots, all running. Cut the Friday newsletter in 2022 and let it stand fallow. Started it again in late 2025 — greener than before.

The founder on her second company

The first company was cut to the stool on purpose, the week the second began. The first is still there, still her, still regrowing. The pole on the third year is straight enough now to lean on.

The painter who used to paint every day

Cut the daily paintings in 2024. The shoots in 2025 were unrecognisably better. The cut was the practice — the practice she had not known she was avoiding by painting every day.
