# Coppice

**The deliberate cut that lets a thing come back stronger.**

A coppice is a managed woodland where trees are cut to the stool
and left to regrow. The cut is not loss. The cut is the practice.
A coppiced ash can outlive an uncut one by a thousand years. The
old wood is the proof of the cutting. Coppice is a small page
for the few things in a life that grow back better for being cut.

## The pitch in one breath

Most planners want a line going up. A coppice is a line going down
on purpose, then up, then down again, on a slow turn. It is the
shape of practice that lasts a long time. The cut is not the end.
The cut is the work.

## The three stands

- **Inner** — what you cut back in yourself: the morning page, the
  evening walk, the practice you fell so it could come back greener.
- **Working** — projects you cut back on purpose: the side book set
  down for a year, the newsletter rested in winter.
- **Edge** — the slow attachments at the edge of your life: the
  Tuesday call, the letter habit, the long Sunday with one person.

## The four states of a stool

- **Drift** — named, but not yet cut. The standing tree, waiting.
- **Shoot** — first regrowth. Green, soft, easy to break.
- **Pole** — straight, useful, ready for the next turn of the cycle.
- **Standard** — left uncut on purpose. Some trees the coppicer
  always leaves. They are the canopy, and the seed, and the proof.

## What's in this folder

- `index.html` — the landing page, with the wood widget in the
  browser.
- `styles.css` — a woodland palette: moss, lichen, hazel, oak, bark,
  ash, hawthorn berry; Fraunces for headings, Inter for body,
  JetBrains Mono for marginalia.
- `script.js` — twelve seeded stools kept by no one in particular
  through a slow turn of the spring of 2026. The wood: a morning
  page standing eight years uncut, a 5K cut and regrowing, a side
  novel left as a standard, a postcard habit named but not yet cut.

## Running it

Open `index.html` in a browser. No build step.
