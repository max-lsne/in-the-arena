# Scion

**Sow a thousand pips and get a thousand wildings. Graft a few and get the tree
you chose.**

A *scion* is a cutting of a tree you already know — this apple, this exact
plum — joined to a strong rootstock so it grows as itself. It is the whole
secret of the orchard, and it is counter-intuitive: a pip from the finest apple
you ever ate does *not* grow that apple. It grows a wilding, a throw of the
genetic dice, sour as often as sweet. The only way to keep a good tree is to
take a cutting of it and graft it on. So the orchardist does not sow the field
and hope. He chooses a few known trees, cuts scions of them to a hardy
rootstock, binds and waxes each union, and walks them every day through the
spring until they knit and grow away. The strength was never in sowing
everything. It was in grafting a chosen few and minding them till they took.

Scion is a small page that holds that shape. It is a board for the few things
you are deliberately grafting onto good stock — the ones you are walking daily —
against the field of chance seedlings flung wide and left to come up as they
will.

## The two axes

A graft is read two ways at once, and they are not the same reading.

**Stage** — how far the union has come:

- **Cut** — sliced clean and matched to the stock, not yet joined.
- **Bound** — set into the stock, bound and waxed at the union.
- **Taking** — callus rising at the join; the two are knitting.
- **Knit** — the union healed; the scion is growing away as itself.

**Condition** — how the graft itself is faring:

- **Dormant** — sealed and still, no life showing yet.
- **Swelling** — a bud fattening; the sap has reached it.
- **Leafing** — first leaves opening; it is drawing for itself.
- **Bearing** — grown into itself and fruiting; the tree you chose.

A graft can be well knitted and still dormant, or pushing leaf hard on a union
that has not yet taken. Minding one axis tells you nothing about the other.

## The bench holds six

The board caps at six unions, and the cap is the whole point. A graft is a wound
kept alive on purpose, and for the weeks it takes to knit it wants close, daily
walking — the binding eased before it strangles, the wax mended, the stock's own
shoots rubbed off. A grafter can only walk so many in a morning. Set the whole
nursery and the far rows go untended and the unions dry. When the bench is full
the next scion waits in the cool, wrapped and dormant, until one has knitted and
grown away on its own.

## Running it

No build step. Open `index.html` in a browser.

- **Graft** a scion with the field, or press <kbd>N</kbd>.
- **Click the bud-mark** to mind it — advance the graft's condition.
- **The row of stages** moves it along the union; the **&times;** lifts it off
  the bench.
- **Rename the nursery** by clicking its name.
- Everything is kept in your browser's `localStorage`. Nothing leaves the
  machine. Double-click the count to clear the bench back to the example.
