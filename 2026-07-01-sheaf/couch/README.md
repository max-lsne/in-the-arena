# Couch

**Steep the whole harvest and the floor moulds. Work a few beds, turned often,
and the malt comes sweet.**

On the malting floor a *couch* is a bed of steeped barley, heaped up so it will
germinate. It is the oldest lesson of the trade: you cannot wet the whole
harvest at once. Do it and the floor heats, the rootlets mat into a felt, the
middle of the heap goes airless, and the lot sours into mould before the week
is out. So the maltster works a *few* couches — steeps a manageable bed,
spreads it thin across the floor, and turns it by hand every few hours so no
part of it heats or mats. Turned like that, the barley chits even, the hard
starch mellows to sweet sugar, and it comes off the kiln as malt. The strength
was never in steeping everything. It was in working a few beds and turning them.

Couch is a small page that holds that shape. It is a board for the few things
you are actually bringing to readiness — the ones you are turning — against the
many you steeped in a burst of enthusiasm and left on the floor to heat.

## The two axes

A couch is read two ways at once, and they are not the same reading.

**Stage** — how far across the floor it has come:

- **Steeped** — just wetted, no life showing, waiting to be couched.
- **Couched** — heaped up, the first rootlet breaking through the husk.
- **Floored** — spread thin and working, turned to keep it even.
- **Kilned** — dried off sweet; the work is malt now, and it keeps.

**Condition** — how it is faring as it works:

- **Slack** — cold and flat, nothing moving.
- **Chitting** — the first rootlet showing; it has caught.
- **Working** — growing both ways, warm, sweetening.
- **Mellow** — grown even and come sweet, ready for the kiln.

A thing can be well onto the floor and gone slack, or chitting hard but only
just steeped. Minding one axis tells you nothing about the other.

## The floor holds six

The board caps at six, and the cap is the whole point. A pair of hands can only
turn so much barley in a shift; past that, the far couches go unturned, the
middle heats, and the whole floor takes the taint. When the floor is full the
next crop stays in the steep until a couch comes off to the kiln. A floor that
tried to work the whole harvest would sour every grain of it.

## The heat

Two of the three pages in this folder carry a live gauge. Couch's reads the
**heat of the floor** — the sum of how much unturned, working grain is heaped
on it at once. Keep it to a tended few and the line lies cool and sweet. Steep
more than you can turn and it climbs toward matting. It is held still when the
browser asks for reduced motion.

## Running it

No build step. Open `index.html` in a browser.

- **Steep** a couch with the field, or press <kbd>N</kbd>.
- **Click the grain-mark** to turn it — advance its condition.
- **The row of stages** moves it across the floor; the **&times;** takes it off
  to the kiln.
- **Rename the floor** by clicking its name.
- Everything is kept in your browser's `localStorage`. Nothing leaves the
  machine. Double-click the count to clear the floor back to the example.
