# Sheaf

**Three small pages, made the same morning, for the same idea seen from three
trades: you bring a few things to readiness by tending them — you do not start
the whole field at once and hope.**

Most days in this folder are one page. Some mornings the idea arrives already
bound in threes. A sheaf is what a reaper makes when the corn is down: you
cannot carry the whole field in your arms, so you gather what one arm will
hold, bind it at the waist, and stand it up in a stook to dry in the wind. A
field cut and left flat on the wet ground goes musty and grows out. The same
corn, taken an armful at a time and bound upright, keeps sweet until the cart
comes. The strength was never in reaping everything at once. It was in binding
a few, standing them together, and letting the air get through.

These three started in different sheds — a maltings, a hedge-line, a grafting
bench — and they meet here, because they are all the one husbandman's habit
said in three trades: **do not start more than you can turn.**

## The three

- **[Couch](./couch/)** — *steep a few beds, and turn them often.* Wet the
  whole harvest at once and the malting floor heats, mats, and moulds by the
  weekend. Steep a few couches, spread them thin, and turn them by hand every
  few hours, and the barley chits even and comes off the kiln sweet. A small
  page for the few works you are actually bringing to malt, turned often —
  against the whole floor steeped at once and left to overheat.
  Steeped → Couched → Floored → Kilned. Slack → Chitting → Working → Mellow.

- **[Pleach](./pleach/)** — *lay a few living stems, don't nail up a fence.* A
  post-and-rail fence is quick and it is dead: it rots, it sags, and in twenty
  years it is a heap. A hedge is laid — you half-cut the stems you choose, bend
  them along the line, and they keep growing into a barrier a bullock can't
  push through, alive for a century. A small page for the few commitments you
  cut, lay, and bind into something living — against the many nailed up quick
  and already rotting.
  Standing → Cut → Laid → Bound. Whippy → Set → Knitting → Stockproof.

- **[Scion](./scion/)** — *graft a few, and mind the union till it knits.* Sow
  a thousand pips and you get a thousand wildings, none of them the tree you
  wanted. Take a few scions of the one good tree, cut them to a strong
  rootstock, bind and wax the union, and mind it through the spring until it
  knits and grows away as the tree you chose. A small page for the few things
  you are deliberately grafting onto good stock — against the field of chance
  seedlings left to come up as they will.
  Cut → Bound → Taking → Knit. Dormant → Swelling → Leafing → Bearing.

## The shared shape

Each page is the same small machine the folder keeps building: a hero, the
problem stated plainly, three steps, an interactive board of a dozen seeded
entries you can sort, advance, and reset, four stages and four conditions, a
handful of people who keep one of these for real, and a quiet waitlist that
promises one email and no streaks. Two of the three carry a live gauge that
reads the whole board at once and only stays calm if you keep it to a tended
few. No build step — open any `index.html` in a browser. It all lives in your
browser; nothing leaves it.
