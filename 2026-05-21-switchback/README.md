# Switchback

**The long way is the way up.**

Switchback is a small page for the climb you can't take straight.
A ridge worth standing on does not have a vertical line to the
top. The trail goes sideways, and sideways again, and sometimes
back down a hundred feet to find the bench that goes up. Each
leg of the climb has a shape and a name. Switchback is for
keeping the trail honest as you cut it.

## The pitch in one breath

Roadmap tools want a straight arrow up. Switchback wants the
truth of the trail — the long traverses, the doubled-back legs,
the storm pinned at the col, the false summit at hour eleven.
One leg at a time. The shape of the climb is the work.

## The three aspects of a leg

- **Up** — a steep leg that takes altitude fast. Short and hard.
- **Across** — a long sideways traverse that gains little. The
  bench you walk to clear an unclimbable face.
- **Back** — a deliberate doubling back. A leg you take down so
  the next one can go up.

## The four states of a leg

- **Scouted** — you have seen the line. You have not walked it.
- **Walking** — you are on it now.
- **Set** — the leg is done. It holds.
- **Redrawn** — the leg did not go. You took it off the map and
  put a new one in its place.

## What's in this folder

- `index.html` — the landing page, with the trail widget in the
  browser.
- `styles.css` — a high-country palette: pine, granite, scree,
  alpenglow, bone; Fraunces for headings, Inter for body,
  JetBrains Mono for marginalia.
- `script.js` — twelve seeded legs from a fictional first ascent
  of an unnamed ridge in the eastern Sangres. Walk a leg, redraw
  one, add the next, reset to the original line.

## Running it

Open `index.html` in a browser. No build step.
