# Bond

**A small page for arranging what you depend on so the fault lines never
run straight through.**

Lay bricks straight, each one directly over the one below, and you have
built a stack of columns, not a wall: every vertical joint runs
unbroken from the footing to the coping, and a crack — from a settling
footing, a frost, a knock — runs the whole height along the first joint
it finds, because nothing was laid across it. The bond is the cure, and
it is nothing but an offset. Lay each brick to bridge the two beneath
it, so no joint ever lines up with the one below, and a load pressing
down at one point must travel out sideways across a dozen bricks before
it reaches the ground. A stack fails where the joint runs. A bonded wall
has nowhere for the crack to run.

## The pitch in one breath

Most plans are stacks. The launch rests on the one hire, who rests on
the one contract, which rests on the one client — a tidy column of
joints, all aligned, and it stands in still weather. But the ground
always settles somewhere, and when it does the crack goes straight down
the line that was never bonded, and one brick takes the whole course
with it. A bond is the same bricks, offset. You lay each commitment to
bridge two others, so no dependency sits squarely on one thing — the
work lapped across two people, the client really three, the revenue
crossing two quarters — and a hard load lands sideways into solid brick
instead of down an open joint. The wall can lose a brick and keep its
course.

## The four bonds

- **Stack** — bricks straight over one another, every joint aligned. No
  lap at all. A row of columns pretending to be a wall; a crack runs the
  full height.
- **Stretcher** — each brick lapped half over the one below. The
  simplest real bond — no joint runs — but one skin thick, easily pushed
  over sideways.
- **English** — whole courses of headers between courses of stretchers,
  tying two skins into one thick wall. Strong, plain, slow.
- **Flemish** — header and stretcher alternating in every course, the
  hard blue headers threaded through the face. The strongest common
  bond, and the one that shrugs off a settling footing.

## The four conditions

- **Green** — just laid, the mortar still wet, gripping nothing yet.
- **Set** — the mortar gone off, the course taking its own weight.
- **Pointed** — the joints raked and struck so the weather runs off, the
  bond finished and sound.
- **Weathered** — years of frost and rain come and gone, still standing
  where a stack would have cracked.

## What's in this folder

- `index.html` — the landing page, with two walls side by side: a
  stack-bonded panel with every joint aligned and a crack running down
  it, and a Flemish-bonded pier with the hard blue headers threaded
  through, each course broken over the last, a plumb line hung between
  them.
- `styles.css` — a brick-and-lime palette: fired terracotta and
  red-brown, lime-mortar cream, hard engineering blue for the strongest
  brick, soot and a cold string-course grey. Fraunces for the headings,
  Inter for the body, JetBrains Mono for the marks.
- `script.js` — twelve stretches of wall across a fictional day at
  Coalpit Row in late August 2026: a canal lock wall in Staffordshire
  blue that has never moved, a garden panel run up stack-bonded with a
  crack down it already, an on-call rota bonded across two engineers, a
  smallholding lapping four incomes over one another.

## Running it

Open `index.html` in a browser. No build step.
