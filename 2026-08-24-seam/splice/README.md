# Splice

**A small page for handing work across by weaving it, not by tying a
quick knot and walking away.**

A knot is the fast join, and it is a poor one. Bent hard around itself,
the rope keeps barely half its strength at the knot, and under a
snatching load a knot can spill — capsize, they call it — and run out to
nothing in an instant. A splice is the slow join. You unlay the strands
of the rope's end and tuck each one back through the standing part, over
one and under one, against the lay, and you do it again, and again —
three tucks at the least, five for a hard-worked line. What you get is
not a lump tied on the end of the rope but a hand-span where the two
parts run woven together, and it holds very nearly the whole strength of
the line and cannot come undone. The difference is entirely in the
overlap: a knot is a join at a point, a splice is a join over a length.

## The pitch in one breath

Most handovers are knots. The leaver writes a page of notes, forwards
the folder, says the names once, and goes — quick, and it looks
finished. It holds until the first real load: the customer who only ever
trusted the one who left, the decision that needed the reason and not
the rule. Then it spills, because half the strength was never carried
across. A splice is the other way. You do not hand the work over at a
line — you unlay it and tuck the leaver's strands back through the
stayer's, over and under, doing the real work side by side, tuck after
tuck, until the two run together over an overlap longer than any single
pull. It costs you an afternoon. It cannot come undone in your hand.

## The four joins

- **Knot** — two ends tied in a hurry. Quick, visible, weak; keeps
  barely half the line and can spill under a shock. The handover done as
  a forwarded folder and a wave.
- **Seizing** — the ends bound side by side with turns of twine. Better
  than a knot, but still two parts held alongside, not yet woven. A
  shared week, no strand tucked through.
- **Short splice** — strands unlaid and tucked back through each other.
  Strong, if thick at the join. The work genuinely handed across, over
  and under, if not yet rolled smooth.
- **Eye splice** — woven, rolled fair, served against chafe. Keeps
  almost the whole strength of the line and cannot come undone. A
  handover you can no longer find the seam of.

## The four conditions

- **Slack** — just tied, gripping nothing yet. The overlap not begun.
- **Tucked** — the first tucks taken, starting to hold. The two carrying
  a little of the load together.
- **Set** — the tucks bedded down under working load. The join holding
  its own now, if not yet smooth.
- **Fair** — rolled underfoot and served, running as smooth as the
  standing part. The seam gone into the body of the rope.

## What's in this folder

- `index.html` — the landing page, with a rigging-loft scene: a
  weathered bollard, the standing part of a manila line leading in, an
  eye splice worked around a galvanised thimble with its three strands
  tucked and the tail whipped, a marlinspike laid across, cold harbour
  water low in the frame.
- `styles.css` — a hemp-and-tar palette: manila and hemp for the
  standing line, tarred marline near-black, whipping-twine cream, a cold
  harbour grey-green. Fraunces for the headings, Inter for the body,
  JetBrains Mono for the marks.
- `script.js` — twelve joins on the bench across a fictional afternoon
  in the rigging loft at Bircham Quay in late August 2026: an eye splice
  the rigger's father laid forty seasons back, a bowline tied in a
  squall, a founder sitting the new lead through six weeks of ruleless
  calls, a mooring strop unlaid to splice again after it spilled.

## Running it

Open `index.html` in a browser. No build step.
