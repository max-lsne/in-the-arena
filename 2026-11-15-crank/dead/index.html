dead centre · noun · the crank position at which the connecting rod and the crank lie in one straight line, so a thrust along the rod yields no turning moment; the stroke's two still points, where the mechanism is, for an instant, dead to force

Dead

Twice in every turn, you can push the slider as hard as you like and the shaft will not move.

A crank turns because the rod's thrust reaches the shaft off to one side of the axis — through a moment arm — and the turn it makes is force times that arm. But the arm is not fixed. At the two ends of the stroke, the crank web, the rod and the line of stroke all fall into one straight line through the axis: the rod now points dead at the centre, its thrust has no sideways reach at all, and the moment arm is zero. These are the dead centres — top and bottom — and on them the slider's push, however great, makes no torque. The torque delivered is the force times the slope of the slider's travel, F·dx/dθ, and that slope passes through zero at each end. A single cylinder stalled on a dead point cannot start itself; it takes a flywheel's stored turning to carry it across.

The bench

Where the push is wasted

Turn the crank and watch two things at once: the mechanism, with the moment arm drawn as the perpendicular from the shaft axis to the rod's line of thrust; and the torque curve for a steady gas force on the slider, swept out over a full revolution. The arm swells to a peak partway down the stroke and collapses to nothing at each end — and where the arm is nothing, the torque is nothing, whatever the force. Those two zeros are the dead centres. Shorten the rod and the curve goes lopsided; the dead points never move.

Driving — the rod's thrust well off the axis, torque near its peak turning

Drag the crank, or let it run. space runs or pauses; ← → step. Stop it on a dead centre and press Push — full force, and the shaft stays put.

What the dead point says

Why a straight line makes no turn

  • Torque is the slider force times the slope of the stroke: T = F·dx/dθ. It is the same slope the throw page drew as the slider's speed — a force delivered where the mechanism is quick, and wasted where it is slow.
  • At each dead centre the rod lies along the line of stroke, straight through the axis. Its thrust has no component across the crank, so the moment arm is zero and dx/dθ = 0: no torque, at any force.
  • There are exactly two per revolution — the far end (outer dead centre) and the near end (inner) — and they are fixed by geometry alone. Changing the rod or the force cannot move them, only reshape the curve between.
  • So a single cylinder delivers torque in pulses with two gaps, and cannot start itself from a gap. The fix is a flywheel: rotation banked during the strong part of the stroke and spent carrying the crank through the dead point.
  • Multi-cylinder engines stagger their cranks so one cylinder is always off a dead centre while another sits on one — the gaps filled by phase, not by storage. One crank, though, has nothing to lean on but its own inertia.

The crank's gift and its flaw are the same fact: it trades a turn for a push through an arm that must, twice a turn, pass through zero. Everything that spins to smooth an engine is there to pay that toll.
