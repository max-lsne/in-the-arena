Lists look back.
Horizons look out.

A backlog is a list of things you owe yourself. A calendar is a list of things you owe other people. Both look behind. Neither one tells you about the weather coming from the open water, or the boat that is still a smudge on the line, or the small thing the tide is bringing in whether you would have chosen it or not.

A headland is the part of the coast that exists to do that work. Headland the page asks the same of a project: stand at the point, look out, name what is coming. Three kinds of arrival. Four states of being seen. A page that reads true the morning the squall actually lands.

How it works

  1. 01

    Walk to the point

    Once a week. Stand somewhere with a wide view of the project — not the desk, not the inbox. The point is a place, not a meeting.

  2. 02

    Sight what is out there

    Squalls, sails, flotsam. Three to ten things in total. The smallest count that still makes the horizon read true.

  3. 03

    Mark what is closing

    A sighting can move closer. Sighted, closing, landed, passed. The four states are a small honesty about what the week is really doing.

  4. 04

    Let most things pass

    Most things pass. Storms slide south, boats make for the next harbor, flotsam drifts back out. A headland that knows this is calmer than a backlog that does not.

The three things on a horizon

  • Squall. Weather you can see arriving. You did not call for it. Worth naming early, even when small — most squalls pass, and the ones that do not are kinder when sighted.
  • Sail. A boat making for harbor. Something with intent — a visit, a launch, a return. It wants to land. The week often turns on which sail makes it in.
  • Flotsam. Small drift on the tide. Not storms, not boats. The unsolicited small things. Sometimes useful, mostly noise. Worth counting so you can stop counting.

Who stands at a point

The studio principal before a quiet month

The sails are the two clients who said they would come back in June. The squalls are the holidays that thin the week and the invoice that has been late twice. The flotsam is the steady small drift of cold emails. The point is the corner office at the back of the studio.

The clinic director before a season change

The squalls are the flu curves rising and the room three repair. The sails are the two new hires coming in on the Monday after Labor Day. The flotsam is the daily drift of vendor mail. The point is the chart room window over the parking lot.

The graduate student in the final fall

The sails are the defense date and the journal decision. The squalls are the funding letter and the family wedding in the same week. The flotsam is the steady drift of conference invitations. The point is the bench by the river behind the lab.

The bakery owner before a long holiday week

The squalls are the heat in the oven shed and the supplier truck that runs Tuesdays. The sails are the two big custom orders and the Saturday market. The flotsam is the inbox of strangers asking for the recipe. The point is the bench by the back door at five.
