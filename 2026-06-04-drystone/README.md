# Drystone

**A small page for the practices that hold by careful fit — without mortar.**

A drystone wall holds because every stone is in conversation with three
others — never forced, always fitted. The Yorkshire wall stands four
hundred years. The Burren walls stand eight centuries. The walls that
last are not the ones bound by glue, but the ones whose fit was patient.
Drystone is a small page for the practices that hold the same way.

## The pitch in one breath

Most planning is mortared — every commitment locked in, scheduled,
joined with glue. A mortared plan is tidy until the ground moves, and
then the whole thing cracks in a line. A drystone is built by fit, not
glue. Each stone earns its place by sitting truly against the three it
touches. The ground moves; the wall shrugs and stays. The fit is the
work.

## The three courses

- **Foundation** — the wide stones at the base: the practices that hold
  up everything else (the morning hour, the Sunday with no work, the
  weekly review that does not get skipped).
- **Wall** — the middle running courses: the steady commitments fitted
  between the base and the top (the Friday close-of-week, the monthly
  mentoring call, the seasonal cull of inboxes).
- **Coping** — the topmost stones that lock the wall down for weather:
  the rituals that finish a day, a quarter, a year (the broom at the
  end of work, the long walk after a hard week, the December retreat).

## The four states of a stone

- **Found** — picked up, weighed in the hand. Not yet placed.
- **Set** — newly fitted. Freshly seated. The first frost will tell.
- **Settled** — held a season or more. The seat has stopped shifting.
- **Lichened** — long stable, grown into the wall. Years held. Mossy.

## What's in this folder

- `index.html` — the landing page, with the drystone widget in the
  browser.
- `styles.css` — a drystone palette: granite, limestone, slate, moss,
  lichen, ochre, rust, chalk; Fraunces for headings, Inter for body,
  JetBrains Mono for marginalia.
- `script.js` — twelve seeded stones kept by no one in particular
  through a slow turn of the summer of 2026. The wall: a 6am run
  lichened nine winters, an end-of-day broom foundation-set
  twenty-two years against the same workshop floor, a Sunday with no
  screens settled three seasons.
