# Purchase

**Three small pages, made the same morning, on one idea seen from three trades:
you do not move a heavy thing by being strong enough to move it. You find
*purchase* — a point to grip and a way to turn a small, steady effort into a
large one — and then you take your time.**

Most days in this folder are one page. Some mornings the idea arrives already
in threes. *Purchase* is a working word before it is a shopping one: it is the
grip and the leverage a thing gives you — the hold a rope takes on a bollard,
the bite a bar takes under a rock, the advantage a block-and-tackle hands back
for the rope you pull through it. A sailor asks for "more purchase" and means a
better hold, not a receipt. The old lesson under all of it is the same: the
strength was never in your arm. It was in the arrangement. Set the arrangement
right and one hand does what ten could not.

There are only three ways to get it, and each of these pages is one:

- **[Sheave](./sheave/)** — *divide the load among the parts.* Reeve a rope
  through more sheaves and the load hangs from more parts of line at once; each
  part carries its share, so the end you haul carries only a fraction — but you
  must haul that many times the rope. Purchase by **division**, and its price is
  distance.

- **[Handspike](./handspike/)** — *multiply by the ratio of the arms.* A bar
  and a fulcrum. Set the fulcrum close under the load and far from your hands,
  and the load's short arm is overpowered by your long one — the advantage is
  simply how much longer your arm is than its. Purchase by **ratio**, and its
  price is travel: your end sweeps far to move the load a little.

- **[Capstan](./capstan/)** — *multiply by the turns you take.* Wrap a rope a
  few times round a drum and the drum's friction eats the load's pull turn by
  turn, so the tail in your hand holds a tiny fraction of what the other end
  drags. Take another turn and the hold you need does not halve — it falls away
  faster than that. Purchase by **exponent**, and it is the cheapest of the
  three: a few turns, and one hand holds tonnes.

## The one shape

Division, ratio, exponent — a fraction, a proportion, and a power. Three
arrangements, three curves, and the same bargain in every one: you never get
force for nothing. The sheave charges you rope; the handspike charges you
reach; the capstan charges you almost nothing, and gives it back as heat in the
rope. What each page does is let you feel where the bargain turns in your
favour — the part, the arm, the turn at which the effort drops under what one
person can actually hold — and lights the band where the load comes home.

Each page is one HTML file, one stylesheet, one script. No build, no account,
no network once it has loaded. The numbers are the real ones: the tackle's
efficiency from a per-sheave loss, the lever from its arms, the drum from the
capstan equation, `T_hold = T_load · e^(−μθ)`.

*One of a series — a page a day, each built on one old working word and the
discipline hidden inside it. 2026-08-01.*
