# Fettle

**The whole heat comes off the mould rough. You can only dress a few of them
sound — so dress those, and let the rest wait by the furnace.**

Nothing leaves a mould finished. A casting comes out of the sand *rough*: the
runners and gates it was poured through still hanging off it, a rind of flash
along the parting line where the two halves of the mould met, burnt sand baked
to the face. To **fettle** is the trade of taking each one to the bench and
dressing it true — saw off the gates, grind the flash back to the line, file
the seam flush, chip the cores out, and then strike it and *listen*: a sound
casting rings a clean note, a flawed one gives back a dull, dead rattle. It is
slow, close work, and it is where a casting actually becomes a part. But a pair
of hands can only true so many in a day. Rush the whole heat through the bench
and the gates get left long, the flash gets called good enough, and the rough
ones pass out with the good.

Fettle is a small page that holds that shape. It is a bench for the few things
you are finishing *properly* — the ones you are dressing to sound before you let
them go — against the many you poured, stacked, and shipped with the flash still
on.

## The two axes

A casting on the bench is read two ways at once, and they are not the same
reading.

**Stage** — how far it has been dressed:

- **Cast** — just out of the mould: gated, sanded, nothing dressed off it yet.
- **Shaken** — knocked out of the flask and set on the bench, gates still on.
- **Fettled** — gates sawn off, flash ground back, the seam filed to the line.
- **Passed** — struck, rung sound, inspected true, and sent on.

**Condition** — how it is faring under the file, right now:

- **Rough** — flash and burnt sand still on it; it rattles dead if you strike it.
- **Grinding** — being worked; the seam is coming off in a shower of sparks.
- **Trued** — filed flat and checked with a rule; clean, but not yet struck.
- **Sound** — struck and heard to ring a clean, true note. Nothing left to do.

The two come apart all the time. A casting can be well *fettled* and still only
*trued* — every gate off and the line clean, but nobody has struck it yet to
hear whether it rings. It can be freshly *cast* and already *grinding*, because
someone set on a rough seam before the piece was even shaken out. How far it is
dressed and how it is faring under the file are different questions, and a good
fettler asks both of every piece on the bench.

## The bench holds six

Fettling does not scale by wanting it to. Every casting on the bench wants the
same slow attention — the gates sawn, the flash ground back to the line, the
seam filed flush, the face struck and listened to. Crowd the bench and the
attention thins out: gates get left long, flash gets called good enough, and a
casting with a hidden draw passes because nobody struck it. So the bench has a
hard ceiling, and the ceiling is the point: **Fettle holds six castings.** When
the bench is full the next one waits by the furnace — cooling, not forgotten —
until one has been passed and sent on. A bench loaded past what one hand can
true would send the rough out with the good.

## The ring

The whole bench is struck as one note. A line across the top of the board is
that note drawn out: it rings a clean, decaying tone when the work on the bench
is sound, and buzzes ragged and dead as the bench fills with rough, half-dressed
castings. It is the fastest read there is — you do not have to walk the bench to
know it is ringing wrong.

## The shape of the page

Hero, the problem stated plainly, three steps, and then the bench: a board of a
few seeded castings you can set on, work, move along the bench, rename, and
reset as you like — each read on both axes. No account, no cloud, no build step.
Everything you change is kept in your browser and nowhere else. Open
`index.html` in a browser; that is the whole of it.
