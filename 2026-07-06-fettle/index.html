What comes off the mould, and what passes

The whole heat comes out rough.
You dress a few of them sound.

Nothing leaves the mould finished. Every casting comes out rough — the runners and gates still hanging off it, a rind of flash along the seam, burnt sand baked to the face. To fettle is to take each one to the bench and dress it true: knock off the gates, grind the flash back to the line, file the seam flush, and strike it to hear whether it rings sound or dead. It is slow, and it is the whole of the work — but a pair of hands can only true so many. Rush the whole heat through and the flash gets left on; the rough ones pass with the good.

How it works

01

Knock it out onto the bench

A thing you mean to finish starts as a casting straight off the mould — rough, gated, sand still on the face. You knock it out of the flask and set it on the bench to be dressed.

02

Dress it, and mind the note

Fettling is slow. You move it along the bench — cast, shaken, fettled, passed — and mind its condition as you go: rough, grinding, trued, sound. How far it's dressed and how it's faring are not the same reading.

03

Strike it and read the ring

A bench holds only so many. Load it past that and work passes with the flash still on. One struck note reads the whole bench — it rings clean when the work is sound and rattles flat when it's rough.

The bench

A bench dresses what one pair of hands can true, and no more.

Fettling does not scale by wanting it to. Every casting on the bench wants the same slow attention — the gates sawn, the flash ground back to the line, the seam filed flush, the face struck and listened to. Crowd the bench and the attention thins out: gates get left long, flash gets called good enough, and a casting with a hidden draw passes because nobody struck it. The cure is never a bigger heat. It is fewer castings, each dressed properly and rung sound before it goes.

So the bench has a hard ceiling, and the ceiling is the point. Fettle holds six. When the bench is full the next casting waits by the furnace — cooling, not forgotten — until one has been passed and sent on. A bench loaded past what one hand can true would send the rough out with the good.
