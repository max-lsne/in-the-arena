# Wether

**A small page about leading by being known, not by being loudest.**

A wether is a quiet, steady sheep — no lambs to fret over, no rut to
win, nothing to prove to the ram across the wall. On an open hill it
is usually the wether that ends up wearing the bell, because it is the
one the flock will actually follow: first to the low ground before the
snow, sure of the ford, settled in a gale. The loud sheep butt heads
at the trough and change nothing. The belled wether walks, and the
whole flock falls in behind it.

## The pitch in one breath

Leadership on the open fell is not noise. There is always a ram that
thinks the flock is a contest — crowding the gate, throwing its weight
about — and the hill is exactly as it was when it has finished. A
wether leads the other way: by being hefted to its ground, by knowing
where the ford is firm and which gill stays green, by being calm on
the worst morning so that following it is the safe and obvious thing
to do. Be known, be hefted, be calm. Then the bell means something.

## The four standings

- **Lamb** — this year's, born on the heft and still at its dam's
  heel. The new start, the first season, the thing that does not yet
  know the ford. Strange to the hill; it would stray if the wall fell.
- **Gimmer** — a young ewe past her first winter, learning the ground
  by running behind the older sheep. Settling in, but not yet sure
  enough to hold the line alone on a bad morning.
- **Ewe** — a settled, hefted breeding sheep that knows her patch by
  heart and teaches it to her lambs. The steady heart of the flock —
  what the place is actually made of.
- **Wether** — the old, calm, belled sheep the whole flock takes its
  bearing from. Nothing to prove and nowhere it doesn't know. A
  lifework, a craft, the quiet thing others follow.

## The four conditions

- **Strange** — newly on the hill and apt to stray, knowing neither
  the fords nor the fold. It needs a wall, or a wether, to hold it.
- **Following** — learning the gathers by running behind the older
  ewes, picking up the route to the fold and the safe crossings.
- **Hefted** — it knows one stretch of open fell by heart and will not
  stray off it even with no wall to hold it. Bred-in knowledge.
- **Leading** — the flock takes its line from this one. On the worst
  morning of the year the others find the low ground by following it.

## What's in this folder

- `index.html` — the landing page, with a hefted flock on a Cumbrian
  fell: the far tops hazed, a drystone wall climbing the slope to
  divide the heft, the scattered pale flock drifting downhill, and the
  bell-wether out front with the bronze bell at its throat.
- `styles.css` — a fell palette: upland mist, wet slate granite, the
  bronze of the bell, dead bracken, late heather, green sward, clean
  fleece, drystone grey. Fraunces for the headings, Inter for the
  body, JetBrains Mono for the seasons.
- `script.js` — twelve head across a fictional fell farm in the
  Cumbrian high country in the back-end of 2026. The old belled wether
  that leads the gather, the grey-faced ewe hefted to the high ghyll,
  this spring's twin lambs still at heel, the gimmer that went over the
  wall in the last gale and never came back.

## Running it

Open `index.html` in a browser. No build step.
