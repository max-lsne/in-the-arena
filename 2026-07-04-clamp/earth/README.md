# Earth

**Heap the whole crop wet and it rots by Christmas. Clamp a sound few under
straw and earth, leave them a breath, and they keep sweet till spring.**

A root *clamp* is how a farmer keeps his roots through the winter without a
cellar: he pulls them, lays a long ridge of them on a bed of straw, thatches
them over with more straw, and covers the lot with a skin of earth patted firm
— but leaves a wisp of straw poking through the crown so the clamp can breathe.
That last thing is the whole craft. Roots are alive; they give off heat and
damp long after they are lifted. Heap the entire crop in, wet and unripe and
airtight, and the middle of the pit sweats, heats, and slimes into rot before
Christmas — the more you saved, the more you lose. So the farmer clamps a
*sound few*: roots that are ripe and dry, laid in a ridge he can walk the length
of, earthed but breathing, and opened a little at a time through the winter so
he can read them for heat and take out any that have turned. The strength was
never in saving everything. It was in clamping a sound few and leaving them a
breath.

Earth is a small page that holds that shape. It is a board for the few things
you are keeping through a fallow season — the ones you have stored right and go
back to read — against the many you heaped in wholesale and left to heat in the
middle of the pit.

## The two axes

A clamp is read two ways at once, and they are not the same reading.

**Stage** — how far into keeping it has come:

- **Pulled** — lifted from the ground, lying out to dry and firm its skin.
- **Strawed** — laid up in a ridge on straw, thatched over against the frost.
- **Earthed** — covered with a skin of earth, a wisp left at the crown to breathe.
- **Opened** — broached in late winter, found sound, and taken in to use.

**Condition** — how it is faring in the clamp, right now:

- **Damp** — freshly pulled, still wet and green, not yet settled.
- **Sweating** — the ridge warming and breathing off; watch it, or it heats.
- **Keeping** — cool, settled, breathing slow; keeping exactly as it should.
- **Sound** — opened and found perfect, kept sweet the whole winter through.

The two come apart all the time. A root can be well *earthed* and still only
*damp* — stored, but not yet settled. It can be freshly *pulled* and already
*sweating* — lifted wet on a warm day and heating before it was ever laid up.
How far into keeping it has come and how it is faring in there are different
questions, and the farmer asks both every time he walks the ridge.

## The clamp holds seven

A clamp is a living store kept on trust for months. It wants walking — the crown
felt for the warm patch that means it is heating in the middle, the breather
kept clear so it does not go airless, the odd turned root drawn out before it
taints its neighbours. That is slow, weekly work, and a farmer can only walk so
many ridges. Heap the whole crop into one great pit and the middle goes
unread, sweats, and rots outward — the more you stored, the more you lose. So
the clamp has a hard ceiling, and the ceiling is the point: **Earth holds seven
roots.** When the clamp is full the next root waits in the field, topped and
drying, until one has been opened and taken in. A pit heaped beyond what one
farmer can walk would heat in the middle before the frosts were even done.

## The shape of the page

Hero, the problem stated plainly, three steps, and then the clamp: a board of a
few seeded roots you can pull, straw, earth, and open, each read on both axes,
renamed and reset as you like. No account, no cloud, no build step. Open
`index.html` in a browser; it all stays in the browser.
