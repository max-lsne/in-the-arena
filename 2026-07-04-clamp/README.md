# Clamp

**Three small pages, made the same morning, for the same idea seen from three
trades: you keep a few things by covering a modest heap and minding it slow —
you do not fire the whole wood at once and hope it comes good.**

Most days in this folder are one page. Some mornings the idea arrives already
banked in threes. A clamp is what you build when you cannot use a whole harvest
at once and cannot leave it lying either: you raise a modest heap, cover it
against the weather, and then you *mind* it — for days, for a season — because
the work is not in the making of the heap but in the tending of it. Build it
too big or leave it unwatched and the middle heats, or fires through, or rots,
and you lose the lot. Build a heap one arm can walk around, cover it, and come
back to read it every day, and it comes good.

Three trades build a thing they each call a clamp, and each learned the same
lesson the hard way. The collier raises a clamp of cordwood, covers it in turf
and dust, and chars it slow — let it draw too much air and it fires through to
white ash. The farmer clamps his roots under straw and a skin of earth for the
winter — heap them wet and unripe and they sweat and rot in the middle. The
limeburner burns a clamp of stone to quicklime and slakes it a little at a time
— slake more than he can knock up and it flashes off, violent and wasted.

They start in different places — a hearth in the wood, a headland by the field,
a kiln by the chalk — and they meet here, because they are all the one husband's
habit said in three trades: **cover only what you can come back and turn.**

## The three

- **[Char](./char/)** — *raise a small clamp, and mind the reek.* Fire a heap
  too big, or leave a blow-hole open, and the clamp draws air, roars, and fires
  through to white ash — a week's cordwood gone in a night. Raise a modest
  clamp, cover it close, and read the smoke: a slow blue reek means it is
  charring inward as it should. A small page for the few things you are turning
  by slow, starved heat — against the whole wood set alight at once and burned
  to ash.
  Raised → Fired → Charring → Drawn. Cold → Smouldering → Reeking → Firing.

- **[Earth](./earth/)** — *clamp a sound few, and leave them a breath.* Heap
  the whole crop, wet and unripe, and the middle of the pit sweats, heats, and
  rots by Christmas. Pull a sound few, lay them on straw, cover them with a skin
  of earth and leave a wisp at the crown to breathe, and they keep sweet till
  spring. A small page for the few things you are keeping through a fallow
  season by storing them right and reading them — against the whole harvest
  heaped in and left to heat.
  Pulled → Strawed → Earthed → Opened. Damp → Sweating → Keeping → Sound.

- **[Slake](./slake/)** — *slake a little, and let it fat in the tub.* Slake
  more quicklime than you can knock up and it boils over, flashes off in a hiss
  of steam, and sets hard and dead — good stone wasted. Slake a little at a
  time, knock it up fat, and leave it to mature in the tub for months and it
  only comes richer. A small page for the few things that only come right slaked
  slow and left to fat — against the whole kiln quenched at once and spoiled.
  Stone → Burnt → Slaked → Fat. Cold → Boiling → Fatting → Rich.

## The shared shape

Each page is the same small machine the folder keeps building: a hero, the
problem stated plainly, three steps, an interactive board of a dozen seeded
entries you can sort, advance, and reset, four stages and four conditions, a
handful of people who keep one of these for real, and a quiet waitlist that
promises one email and no streaks. Two of the three carry a live gauge that
reads the whole board at once and only stays calm if you keep it to a tended
few. No build step — open any `index.html` in a browser. It all lives in your
browser; nothing leaves it.
