# Char

**Fire the whole wood at once and it burns to ash. Raise a few small clamps,
mind the reek, and the wood comes off as charcoal.**

A charcoal *clamp* is a heap of cordwood stacked close, lit at the core, and
covered over with turf and earth so almost no air can reach the fire. That is
the whole craft: charcoal is what you get when wood is made to burn *starved* —
hot enough to drive off the water and gas, too smothered to flare into flame.
Give it air and it does the easy thing: it roars, fires through, and by morning
a week's cordwood is a heap of white ash and nothing to sell. So the collier
does not light the whole wood. He raises a *few* modest clamps — heaps he can
walk around and reach the top of — covers them close, and then he *reads the
reek*: a slow blue smoke means it is charring inward as it should; a thin white
plume means it has found air and is firing through, and he must dust the leak
and starve it back down. The strength was never in burning everything. It was
in raising a few clamps and minding the smoke.

Char is a small page that holds that shape. It is a board for the few things
you are turning by slow, starved heat — the ones you are actually minding —
against the many you set alight in a burst of enthusiasm and left to fire
through to ash.

## The two axes

A clamp is read two ways at once, and they are not the same reading.

**Stage** — how far the burn has come:

- **Raised** — cordwood stacked and covered, not yet lit; a heap waiting.
- **Fired** — the core lit through the flue, the clamp taken light.
- **Charring** — burning slow and inward, a steady blue reek; the working stage.
- **Drawn** — burned through, cooled under its cover, and drawn as charcoal.

**Condition** — how the fire is faring, right now:

- **Cold** — no fire in it, or gone out; nothing moving.
- **Smouldering** — a low, safe smoulder, barely a wisp.
- **Reeking** — a good steady blue reek; charring well, exactly right.
- **Firing** — a thin white plume; it has found air and is running to ash.

The two come apart all the time. A clamp can be well into *charring* and still
only *smouldering* — slow, but safe. It can be freshly *fired* and already
*firing through* — a blow-hole found the wind before the char had set. How far
it has come and how hard it is burning are different questions, and a collier
asks both every time he walks the hearth.

## The hearth holds five

A clamp is a fire kept alive on a knife-edge for days. It wants walking — the
cover patched where it cracks, the leaks dusted before they draw, the whole
dome felt for the hot spot that means it is firing through underneath. That is
close, hourly work, and a collier can only walk so many clamps in a night. Fire
the whole wood at once and the far heaps go unwatched, find air, and burn to
ash — good cordwood wasted for want of minding. So the hearth has a hard
ceiling, and the ceiling is the point: **Char holds five clamps.** When the
hearth is full the next stack waits in the cord, cut and dry, until one has
charred through and been drawn. A hearth lit beyond what one collier can walk
would fire through, heap by heap, before the week was out.

whole hearth at once — calm and blue when the clamps char slow, flaring white
