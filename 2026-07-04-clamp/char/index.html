What comes of slow, starved heat

Fire the whole wood,
and you get a heap of ash.

A charcoal clamp is a heap of cordwood, lit at the core and covered close in turf and earth so barely any air can reach the fire. That is the whole craft: charcoal is wood made to burn starved — hot enough to drive off the water and the gas, too smothered to break into flame. Give it air and it does the easy thing: it roars, fires through, and by morning a week's cordwood is white ash. So you do not light the whole wood. You raise a few modest clamps, cover them close, and read the reek — a slow blue smoke means it chars inward as it should; a thin white plume means it has found air, and you dust the leak and starve it back down. A few clamps, walked and minded, against the whole wood set alight at once.

How it works

Stop firing the whole wood. Raise a few clamps and read the reek.

01

Raise it, and light the core

A thing you mean to turn by slow heat starts as a raised clamp — cordwood stacked close and covered over — then lit at the core through a flue, so the fire starts where the air can't feed it.

02

Char it, and read the smoke

A clamp is minded by reading it. You move it along — raised, fired, charring, drawn — and you read how it fares: cold, smouldering, reeking, firing. How far it has burned and how hard it is burning are not the same thing.

03

Starve it before it fires through

A clamp lives or dies by its cover. Raise more than you can walk and a blow-hole finds the wind unnoticed, the dome roars, and the char burns to ash. A few, felt for hot spots and dusted where they leak, char through and draw as charcoal.

The hearth

A collier walks only as many clamps as he can reach the top of in a night.

A clamp is a fire kept on a knife-edge for days. For the whole burn it wants walking — the cover patched where it cracks, the leaks dusted before they draw, the dome felt all over for the hot spot that means it is firing through underneath. That is close, hourly work, and a collier can only walk so many heaps in a night. Light the whole wood at once and the far clamps go unwatched, find air, and burn to white ash — good cordwood wasted for want of a hand on it. The cure is never more clamps. It is fewer, raised where you will pass them, and read every hour.

So the hearth has a hard ceiling, and the ceiling is the point. Char holds five clamps. When the hearth is full the next stack waits in the cord, cut and dry, until one has charred through and been drawn. A hearth lit beyond what one collier can walk would fire through, heap by heap, before the week was out.

Who keeps one

For people who set more alight than they can walk.

The whole-wood lighter

A dozen things set going in one bright burst, all of them roaring, none of them watched. Char asks which five you are actually minding, and lets the rest wait in the cord.

The slow turner

A craft, a manuscript, a grief — things that only come good when they are held at a slow, starved heat and turned inward for a long while. Char is built for the long, banked burn.
