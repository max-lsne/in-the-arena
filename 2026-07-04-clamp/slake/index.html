What comes of slaking slow and letting it fat

Quench the whole kiln,
and it sets hard and dead.

Lime is burned in a clamp kiln to quicklime, and quicklime is a hungry thing: add water and it doesn't just wet, it slakes — it boils, hisses, throws steam, and swells to twice its bulk as it turns to putty for mortar and plaster. Get greedy and tip water over the whole kiln at once and it flashes off in a violent boil, burns off its strength as steam, and sets hard and dead in the pit — a week's stone wasted. So you slake a little at a time: a barrowful into the tub, water run in slow, knocked up to a smooth fat putty, and then — the part no one has patience for — left. Lime putty only improves with keeping. A little, slaked and fatted, against the whole kiln quenched at once.

How it works

Stop quenching the whole kiln. Slake a little and let it fat.

01

Burn it, and draw the quicklime

A thing you mean to bring right by slaking starts as stone, burned in the kiln to quicklime and drawn off hungry — caustic, live, and ready for the tub, not yet touched with water.

02

Slake it, and read its temper

Slaking is minding a boil. You move it along — stone, burnt, slaked, fat — and you read how it tempers: cold, boiling, fatting, rich. How far along it has come and how hot it is running are not the same thing.

03

Leave it to fat before it flashes off

A batch is made in the keeping. Slake more than you can knock up and the tubs boil over unwatched, flash off their strength, and set dead. A few, knocked up fat and left long under water, come back richer than the day they were slaked.

The yard

A limeburner minds only as many tubs as he can keep from flashing off.

Slaked lime is a live thing kept under water for months. It wants tending — the tub kept topped so the putty never dries and carbonates, the fresh boil watched so it does not flash off, the matured fat knocked up before it stiffens. That is slow, patient work, and a limeburner can only mind so many tubs. Quench the whole kiln at once and the far tubs boil over unwatched, flash off their strength, and set dead in the pit — the more stone you slaked, the more you spoiled. The cure is never more tubs. It is fewer, set where you'll pass them, and knocked up in their own time.

So the yard has a hard ceiling, and the ceiling is the point. Slake holds six tubs. When the yard is full the next barrow waits as quicklime, dry and covered, until one tub has fatted and been knocked up and used. A yard slaked beyond what one burner can walk would flash off, tub by tub, before the mortar was ever wanted.

Who keeps one

For people who slaked more than they can knock up.

The whole-kiln quencher

A dozen things all doused at once in a burst, boiling over and flashing off unwatched. Slake asks which six you are actually knocking up, and lets the rest wait as dry quicklime.

The slow maturer

A craft, a mortar, a temper — things that only come rich when they are slaked slow and left long under water, better for the keeping. Slake is built for the months in the tub.

The one who flashed a batch off

You ran the water in too fast, or too much at once, and a good tub boiled over, spat off its strength, and set hard and dead. This is for slaking a little and standing over each boil.
