# Slack

**Three small pages, made the same morning, on one idea seen from three trades:
*slack* is not a failure to pull tight. It is a quantity you design — the
measured give a working thing needs to survive a shock, a reversal, or the
heat of the day. Leave too little and it snatches, binds, or buckles. Leave
too much and it wanders, slops, and clatters. The craft is finding the band
between the two walls, because both walls break.**

Most days in this folder are one page. Some mornings the idea arrives already
in threes. *Slack* is a word we use as an insult — *take up the slack*, *a
slacker* — as if give were only ever waste. But every load-bearing thing anyone
ever trusted was built with give left in on purpose, and the amount was not
guessed. Too taut is not the safe end of the scale; it is its own failure,
usually the more sudden one. A rope with no stretch snaps at the first gust. A
gear with no clearance seizes when it warms. A rail with no gap kinks in the
sun and throws the train. The discipline under all three is the same: decide how
much give, measure it, and defend both edges of the band.

There are three kinds of give here, and each of these pages is one:

- **[Rode](./rode/)** — *give measured as length.* An anchor chain is paid out
  long on purpose so it lies in a deep curve — a catenary — and pulls the anchor
  flat along the seabed while the sag soaks up every surge. Too short a scope and
  the rode comes up straight, lifts the anchor, and snatches it out; too long and
  the boat sails a wide circle and fouls the anchorage. Give measured in **scope**,
  and its wall is the angle at the anchor.

- **[Backlash](./backlash/)** — *give measured as clearance.* Gear teeth are cut
  a hair loose so they never bind — so they can carry oil, shrug off dirt, and
  expand when they warm without cooking themselves solid. But that same gap is
  lost motion the instant you reverse: the driven tooth free-falls across it
  before it bites. Too tight and the train jams hot; too loose and it hammers and
  loses its place. Give measured in **thousandths**, and its wall is the reversal.

- **[Breath](./breath/)** — *give measured against heat.* A rail, a bridge, a
  steam pipe is laid with a gap — room to breathe — because steel grows with the
  day and something has to yield. Close the gap too small and the summer sun turns
  growth it cannot make into a force that buckles the rail; leave it too big and
  the winter joint gapes and hammers every wheel that crosses. Give measured in
  **millimetres against degrees**, and its wall is the buckling force.

## The one shape

Length, clearance, heat-room — three quantities, three trades, and the same
bargain in every one: give is not the opposite of strength, it is part of it,
and it has a right value with a failure on each side. Set it too small and the
thing you built to bend instead breaks all at once — the snatched anchor, the
seized gear, the kinked rail. Set it too large and it survives, but sloppily —
it wanders, it slops, it clatters, and it wears itself out at the joint. What
each page does is let you feel where the good band sits and how narrow it is —
and light the moment the give stops being slack and starts being the design.

Each page is one HTML file, one stylesheet, one script. No build, no account,
no network once it has loaded. The numbers are the real ones: the catenary of a
weighted rode, the lost motion and bind margin of a meshed pair, the thermal
strain `α·ΔT` against a rail's buckling force.
