# Breath

**Leave the rail a gap to grow into, or the summer sun turns the growth it cannot
make into a force that buckles it. Leave too much and the winter joint gapes and
hammers every wheel that crosses.**

Steel does not know how long you meant it to be. Warm it and it grows; the
amount is not small, and if the rail is pinned and cannot grow, every millimetre
of length it was denied comes back as force — enormous force, because the same
stiffness that makes steel useful makes it a terrible spring. So a rail, a bridge
deck, a run of steam pipe is laid with a *gap*: a designed slack, room to breathe,
sized against the hottest day the metal will ever see. Too small, and the heat it
cannot spend as growth it spends as thrust, and the rail throws itself sideways —
a sun kink. Too large, and the joint gapes all winter and hammers every wheel.

## The one catch

The same gap that lets the rail breathe in the heat is an open mouth in the cold:

- **Too small a gap — it buckles.** The gap closes before the peak, the denied
  growth becomes thrust, and the rail throws itself out of line.
- **The working band — breathing.** The gap is sized to the swing: nearly shut at
  the peak, a little open at the trough, never building real thrust.
- **Too large a gap — it clatters.** The joint gapes the year round and every
  wheel drops into it and climbs out, hammering the rail-ends.

The cure for a rail that buckles is not a stronger rail — a stronger one buckles
at nearly the same temperature — it is more gap, or holding it neutral so it
needs none. The cure for a joint that clatters is a gap no wider than the heat
demands.

The bench uses the thermal law directly:

```
ΔL = α · L · ΔT           σ = E · (ΔL − gap) / L
```

where `α` is the material's expansion, `L` the span, `ΔT` the swing, and `E` the
modulus. The gap must be at least `ΔL`, and whatever it denies returns as
compressive stress until the rail buckles.

## The bench

One expansion joint from the side: two rail-ends on their sleepers, the gap
between, and a thermometer running the day from cold to hot. Set the **gap**, the
**span**, the **heat**, and the **material**. The page shows the growth the heat
demands, the thrust when the gap runs out, and the open joint left in the cold,
and lights the **breathing band**.

It opens on a gap cut too small for a long steel span in a hard summer — the
rail buckling. **Cut the gap wider** — and watch the thrust fall away before it
kinks.
