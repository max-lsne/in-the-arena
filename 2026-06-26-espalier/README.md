# Espalier

**A small page about training a thing flat against a wall, a few degrees a
season, until the shape stops being effort and becomes the tree.**

Espalier is the slow craft of training a fruit tree flat against a wall —
one cut, one tie, one season at a time — into a shape it would never take
on its own. A maiden left alone puts up a round, tangled, leggy bush that
shades its own fruit and snaps in the wind. Pruned and tied to the wire,
the same tree lays its arms out level, ripens every apple in the sun, and
lives twice as long. You cannot do it in a weekend. The wood only learns
slowly, and a branch bent too fast just breaks.

## The pitch in one breath

A tree is not shaped by how hard you bend it but by how patiently. Bend a
limb the whole way down in one afternoon and it cracks at the curve or
sulks and throws water-shoots straight for the sky. Bend it a few degrees
this winter, tie it, and wait; a few more next winter; prune in summer
back to the spur to send the strength into fruit instead of leaf. Season
after season the wood lignifies into the bend, the string comes off, and
one day the tree simply grows flat and fruitful against the wall and has
forgotten it ever wanted to be a bush. Bend it a little. Tie it in. Wait
for the wood.

## The four stages of a trained limb

- **Whip** — a bare maiden shoot of its first year, all sap and reach and
  no shape. Left alone it makes a round bush. Pure potential, pure risk;
  the new intention, not yet bent to anything.
- **Cordon** — a single arm bent down to the first wire and tied, learning
  the horizontal. It has a direction now, but the wood has not set; cut
  the string and it springs back. The habit taking a shape.
- **Tier** — a second and third arm laid in and summer-pruned to spurs;
  the form is legible and beginning to fruit. Properly trained, but still
  wants the yearly cut. The discipline with structure.
- **Frame** — the whole espalier set and self-supporting, the string long
  gone, fruiting on the same spurs every year. It stopped being trained
  and became the shape the tree grows in. The lifework.

## The four things the wood is doing

- **Leggy** — grown out, untied, reaching back toward the bush it wants to
  be. Wants the secateurs.
- **Tied** — bent to the wire and tied, but the wood has not set. Held by
  string, not yet by itself.
- **Pruned** — summer-cut back to the spur, the strength directed into
  fruit. The deliberate cut made this season.
- **Set** — the wood has lignified into the bend and holds the shape with
  no string at all. It simply grows that way now.

## What's in this folder

- `index.html` — the landing page, with an old warm-brick south wall and a
  tree trained flat against it: the trunk low, the arms laid out level on
  the wires, fruit on the spurs, and one limb gone leggy and bolting up off
  the wire toward the bush it wants to be.
- `styles.css` — a walled-garden palette: limewash and old mortar, espalier
  leaf-green, warm south-wall brick, trained bark, spring blossom,
  galvanised wire, the gold of a ripe apple. Fraunces for the headings,
  Inter for the body, JetBrains Mono for the seasons.
- `script.js` — twelve limbs on one fictional garden's board in the
  back-end of 2026: the old pear fruiting on its spurs, the maiden whip cut
  this spring, the water-shoot that bolted and was never stopped, the
  espalier a previous gardener let grow out into a bush. A live fettle gauge
  reads the whole wall — a limb that lies flat along the wire when the
  growth is trained and set, and lifts up toward the bush the more you leave
  leggy and uncut.

## Running it

Open `index.html` in a browser. No build step.
