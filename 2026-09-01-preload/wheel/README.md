# Wheel

**A small page for the joint held clamped before the cart ever rolls —
the wheelwright shrinks the tyre so hard that the road can only relax
what the fire drove tight.**

A wheel carries nothing by the strength of its spokes. Cut the tyre and
the spokes stand loose in their sockets, and the first pothole shakes the
whole thing to a heap of sticks. What carries the cart is done to the
wheel before it ever turns: the wheelwright **shrinks the tyre on**. The
iron is forged a shade too small — a full turn too short. Heated to a
dull red it swells just enough to drop over the felloes; quenched, it
snaps back and crushes the whole wheel inward, driving every spoke tight
into its mortise. The wheel stands in a great ring of **compression**
before a pound of cart is on it. That is the **preload**.

## The pitch in one breath

A spoke is only *pushed* into a mortise, and a mortise ever allowed to
go loose — even for an instant — begins to hammer: the spoke knocks on
every turn, wears its shoulders round, splits the felloe, and the wheel
unravels from that one joint. So the wheelwright never lets a joint go
slack. He shrinks the tyre so hard that every joint begins clamped, and
arranges that the worst a rolling load can do is clamp it a little
*less*. The load comes down through the hub and, at the bottom of the
wheel, comes straight off the preload — the spoke is *unloaded, never
pulled slack*. A whole life of pounding is answered once, at the fire.

## The one relation

Take the loaded bottom spoke and its felloe as two springs pulling
against each other — the iron rim in tension, the spoke and its joint in
compression, both set to preload `F₀` when the tyre shrinks on. The cart
adds a load `P`; rim and spoke share the give, so only `φ = k_rim/(k_rim
+ k_spoke)` reaches the rim and the rest comes off the spoke's clamp:

- **`F_rim = F₀ + φ·P`** — the iron feels only the small share `φ` of the
  cart, so a tyre shrunk on taut barely fatigues over a life of turns.
- **`F_spoke = F₀ − (1−φ)·P`** — the clamp that jams the spoke home, and
  the number that must never reach zero.
- **`P_sep = F₀ /(1−φ)`** — the load at which the clamp reaches zero and
  the spoke goes slack. Below it, jammed home; at it, it knocks.

The **preload** `F₀` sets how heavy a cart the wheel can carry before a
spoke goes slack; the **split** `φ` sets how much of the road's pounding
the iron itself has to feel. It is the same trick the wire wheel plays
upside down — the rim squeezed, the spokes strung in tension — so that a
bicycle stands not *on* its bottom spokes but *hung from the top*, the
bottom ones merely let off their tension by the load. Preload, either
way round.

## The bench

A wheel face-on: hub, spokes to a ring of felloes, the iron tyre shrunk
round the outside, and the bottom spoke — where the load lands — marked.
Set how hard the tyre is **shrunk** on (the preload `F₀`) and the
**split** `φ`; press **Load** to bring the cart's weight onto the axle
and watch the clamp bleed off the bottom spoke. Below, the **joint
diagram**: the rim's tension rising gently and the bottom spoke's clamp
falling steeply, crossing zero at `P_sep` — after which the spoke is
slack and knocking. Keep the load left of that line and the wheel stays
sound.
