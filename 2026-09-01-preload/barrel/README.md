# Barrel

**A small page for the seam that is held shut before a drop is poured —
the cooper drives the hoops so hard that the load, when it comes, has
nothing left to break.**

A barrel holds nothing by its wood. Stand the staves in a ring and they
clatter to the floor: there is no glue down the seam, no groove, nothing
between one stave and the next but a planed edge. What holds a cask
together — and holds it *watertight* — is done to it before it ever meets
a drop. The cooper drives the **hoops**: iron rings cut a shade too
small, so that driven down the barrel's swell each one must stretch to
pass, and stretched it pulls back, squeezing every stave against its
neighbours. Every vertical joint begins its life in **compression**.
That built-in squeeze is the **preload**.

## The pitch in one breath

A butted edge in tension is the weakest thing there is — the moment it is
pulled open by a hair, the seam is a channel and the cask is spoiled. So
the cooper never lets the joint into tension at all. He spends a great
compression into the wood in advance, by hand, and arranges that the
worst the wine can do is squeeze the seam a little *less*. Fill the cask
and the pressure pushes the staves outward — but that push does not start
from nothing and break the joint; it starts by eating into the squeeze
the hoops already built. The contact stays a contact. The seam is won
not at the waterline but at the workbench.

## The one relation

Model a seam as two springs pulling against each other — the hoop in
tension, the ring of staves in compression, both set to the preload
`F₀` when the hoops go on. The fill adds an outward load `P`; hoop and
staves share the stretch, so only a fraction `φ = k_hoop/(k_hoop +
k_stave)` reaches the hoop and the rest comes off the squeeze:

- **`F_hoop = F₀ + φ·P`** — the iron feels only the small share `φ` of
  the fill, so a hoop set up taut barely notices the wine and barely
  fatigues under the endless swell and shrink.
- **`F_joint = F₀ − (1−φ)·P`** — the squeeze that holds the seam, and the
  one that runs out. This is the number that matters.
- **`P_sep = F₀ /(1−φ)`** — the fill at which the squeeze reaches zero
  and the seam lifts. Below it, watertight, and the wood never sees a
  tensile pound. At it, the cask weeps.

Two lessons: the **preload** `F₀` sets how much fill the seam can swallow
before it opens, and the **split** `φ` sets how much of the load's endless
flutter the iron has to feel. Set `F₀` so `P_sep` sits comfortably past
the fullest the cask will ever be, and keep `φ` small with a giving hoop
against stiff staves, and the seam is perfect for years.

## The bench

A cask in elevation, its staves stood in a ring and its hoops driven on,
with one central seam marked. Set how hard the hoops are **driven** (the
preload `F₀`) and the **split** `φ`; press **Fill** to raise the load and
watch the wine climb, the staves pushed out, and the squeeze bleed off
the marked seam. Below, the **joint diagram**: the hoop force rising
gently and the seam's squeeze falling steeply as the cask fills, crossing
zero at `P_sep` — after which the seam is sprung and weeping. Keep the
fill left of that line and the cask stays tight.
