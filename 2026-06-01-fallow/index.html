The rest is
the work.

A fallow is a field deliberately left unsown for a season — a year, sometimes two, sometimes seven — so the soil can recover from being worked. The Romans rotated thirds. The medieval English rotated halves. The longest-kept fields in the world have always been the ones that were also, regularly, the longest-rested. Fallow is a small page for the work that wants the same.

Planners
are for harvests.

Most planners want every field worked every season — every project shipping, every newsletter weekly, every notebook open. A planner is a beautiful instrument until the soil wears out — and then the planner is the last to notice.

A fallow is a quiet field on purpose. It is the slowest shape in the working year, and the most patient — and when the next sowing comes, the soil is deeper for the rest. The hedges grew in. The skylarks came back. The rest kept the field. The rest is the work.

How it works

  1. 01

    Name a field

    One thing — a project, a habit, a public presence — you mean to set down for a season. Not a quit. A fallow.

  2. 02

    Pick a land

    Field for the central work. Margin for the smaller things at the edges. Hedgerow for the boundary practices you keep year-round.

  3. 03

    Let it rest

    A field begins standing. Then it's set down at the cut. Then it rests deeper. Then — sometimes — it becomes sward, long-fallow, a stable quiet of its own.

  4. 04

    Don't plough back early

    A field ploughed back in the panic of the second month is no field rested. Keep the hedge. The slow restoring is what makes the next sowing worth it.

The three lands

  • Field. The central work you have set down: a book between drafts, a company in the year after sale, a research line waiting on its slow data.
  • Margin. The smaller things at the edges, set down on purpose: a newsletter rested for a winter, a photo feed left to grow over, a conference circuit you stopped traveling for.
  • Hedgerow. The boundary practices you keep year-round to mark what's resting: a Sunday with no work, a quarterly check-in, the long walk where the field is in sight but you do not enter it.

Who keeps a fallow

The novelist between the second and third book

The second book is set down a year, the third is standing in the field. The Sunday with no screens is a hedgerow eleven years tall. The newsletter is a margin sward, gone over to grass since 2021. She is not behind. She is rotating.

The founder a year after the sale

The company is a field, set down at exit. A consulting line is standing. Wednesday afternoons in the garden have rested four years now. The advisory calls he cut in 2023 have gone sward. The hedge has thickened around the field.

The researcher between two long datasets

One paper is in the field, resting two years on slow data. The Twitter feed is a margin gone sward — three years since the last post. The teaching is standing. The quarterly call with a co-author is a hedgerow she keeps in every weather.

The painter on the long quiet practice

The Instagram feed is set down — bare since January. The big oils are resting four years. The small daily watercolours are standing, the only field she works. The sward in the margin is what makes the field bear.
