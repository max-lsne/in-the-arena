Your half-thoughts deserve a hearth.

You open Apple Notes. You see 412 entries titled (no title). You close Apple Notes.

Capture is the easy half. What's missing is the second half — a small, weekly ritual that says this one's still warm, this one's ash, both are fine. Tinderbox is built around that second half. Capture is the on-ramp; the sift is the point.

The ritual

  1. 01

    Drop a spark

    One line. A phrase, a hunch, a half-question. No title field, no folder picker. Three taps from lock screen to logged.

  2. 02

    Pick a flavor

    Line, image, or hunch. That's it. Three flavors keep the on-ramp short and the sift legible later. Re-flavoring is fine.

  3. 03

    Sunday: sift the ash

    Sunday morning, 15 minutes. Each spark from the week gets one verdict — tend (it's kindling now) or let go (it's ash, no shame). Most are ash. That's the whole shape.

  4. 04

    Tend the kindling

    Kindling gets one more Sunday. If it's still warm, it graduates to an ember — a thing you're actively working on. Embers leave the box. The box stays small.

What the verdicts mean

  • Spark. Fresh capture. Less than a week old. No commitment, no obligation. Most sparks die here, and that's the design.
  • Kindling. Survived one Sunday sift. Still warm. Gets one more week to prove it's not a passing mood.
  • Ember. Graduated to active work. Leaves the box and joins your real tools — a Scrivener project, a sketchbook, a repo.
  • Ash. Let go on purpose. Not a failure — a finished thought. Ash is most of what a healthy box produces.

Who keeps a tinderbox

The novelist mid-draft

Half a book in. Lines arrive on walks, in queues, between scenes. The box catches them so the page doesn't have to.

The designer between briefs

Visual hunches from a museum trip, a film, a tile floor. Most won't fit a project. The few that do, do.

The researcher with a hunch problem

You have eight half-questions a day, none of which justifies a doc. The box holds them cheaply, the sift keeps the few worth a doc.

The slow founder

Product ideas, hires, copy lines, gnawing doubts. You don't need them sorted today. You need them somewhere you'll actually look on Sunday.
